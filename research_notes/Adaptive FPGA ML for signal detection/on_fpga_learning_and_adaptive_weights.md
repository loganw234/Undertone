# On-FPGA Training, On-Chip/Online Learning, and Runtime-Adaptable Weights on FPGA Hardware

Research date: 2026-11-21. Target reader context: small, very-low-latency model on an Alveo U50 (UltraScale+, 8 GB HBM) that keeps learning in the field.

Verification legend used throughout:
- no tag = numbers read from the paper's own text/tables (HTML full text or PDF text extraction).
- **[S]** = snippet/abstract-level only (search-result summary or abstract page); the full paper was not read, treat numbers as unverified.
- **[2nd]** = number taken from another paper's comparison table, not from the primary source.

## 1. Full training on FPGA (backpropagation / SGD), 2018-2026

### Takeaway
Full backprop on a single FPGA is well demonstrated for CIFAR-10-class CNNs at roughly 20-200 GOPS at batch 1 and 4-20 W, almost always with 8-bit operands plus wider (16-48 bit) accumulators or shared exponents; training costs about 3x the MACs of inference plus activation storage, and 2-15x the logic of an inference-only core when fully unrolled. For tiny MLP/KAN-class models, fully unrolled per-sample SGD has been shown at 50-140 ns per update (Virtex UltraScale+, 2026) and about 1-2.5 µs per update (Kintex-7/Artix-7, 2018), which is the regime relevant to a low-latency U50 prototype.

### Inferences
- Precision rule of thumb across these works: 8-bit operands are enough for forward and backward passes if (a) there is a per-block/layer shared exponent or shift (block FP, block minifloat, NITI shifts) and (b) the weight store or gradient accumulator is wider (16-48 bits). Plain low-precision fixed-point BP without rescaling fails early (Q2.8 BP failed where WP still trained; the 32-16-8-16-32 autoencoder needed 14 bits).
- Logic overhead of on-chip BP depends strongly on architecture: fully unrolled per-sample BP is 2-4x LUTs and up to 15x FFs of the inference core (FPT'18), while time-shared CNN trainers mostly pay in BRAM for activations (BOOST: 63-66% BRAM saved when only the last layers train).
- For models in the 100-1,500-parameter range, an unrolled HLS SGD datapath costs only a few thousand to about 10k LUTs and around 100 DSPs on UltraScale+ and updates in under 200 ns; this is a small fraction of a U50-class device, so logic area is not the binding constraint for the reader; numerical stability and label/target availability are.
- NITI's U280 prototype is the closest published use of an HBM-equipped Alveo card for training, but only the arithmetic kernels were characterized.

### Gaps
- DarkFPGA's own throughput/resource table was not read directly; the row above comes from BOOST's comparison table. Whether DarkFPGA's INT8 scheme is formally WAGE-derived was not verified. No stand-alone FPGA implementation paper of WAGE itself was found.
- FeCaffe benchmark numbers and source availability were not found.
- EF-Train LUT counts and per-image latency were not retrieved (page truncated).
- No published on-FPGA training work targeting the Alveo U50 specifically was found.

## 2. Backpropagation-free and local learning rules in FPGA hardware

### Takeaway
The BP-free methods with real FPGA implementations are weight perturbation (MLPs), DFA-style and three-factor local rules in spiking networks, e-prop for recurrent SNNs, STDP, Tsetlin-automaton feedback, and layer-local losses for CNNs; they typically cost +20% to +110% logic over inference and lose about 1-10 accuracy points versus backprop on MNIST/CIFAR-class tasks. Forward-Forward has only a bachelor-thesis FPGA implementation, zeroth-order/SPSA/forward-gradient methods have only RNG-block or MCU implementations, and no FPGA implementation of equilibrium propagation or target propagation was found.

### Cited Findings

**Weight perturbation / zeroth-order / forward gradients**
- Weight perturbation on FPGA trains with finite differences by re-using the inference datapath; at Q2.8 WP reached 94.7% on Iris where BP reached 52% (Q2.10: 94.7% vs 85.3%); authors attribute BP failure to error accumulation in the fixed-point backward pass. Softmax was avoided "as softmax is highly susceptible to both under and overflow in fixed-point arithmetic". For the RF anomaly autoencoder both methods needed at least 14 bits (Q2.12) and matched floating-point F1 (0.57) — [Siddhartha et al., FPT'18](https://phwl.github.io/assets/papers/wp_fpt18.pdf)
- PeZO (2025): identifies random-number generation as the main hardware obstacle for zeroth-order training; a MeZO baseline with 1024 Gaussian RNGs costs 133,120 LUTs (48.6% of a ZCU102) and 4.474 W, while PeZO's on-the-fly scheme (pool of about 2^5 uniform RNGs with rotation and power-of-two scaling) costs 32 LUT / 449-512 FF / 1 BRAM and 0.608-0.626 W. Naive uniform or Rademacher perturbations collapsed to about 50% on SST-2 in their setting. Only the RNG block was synthesized (ZCU102, Vivado 2020.1); accuracy experiments were software on language models; no end-to-end FPGA trainer — [PeZO arXiv 2504.20314](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20314v2)
- Quantized zeroth-order training on MCUs (Zhao et al., 2024; later ACM TECS per search): Rademacher perturbations, quantized forward passes only; 668 KB versus 3,650 KB memory for BP on MCUNet; 6.4% average accuracy gain over quantized BP on corrupted-image adaptation; but per-step latency is far higher (72.8 s BP-free on TinyEngine versus 13.4 s BP on TF-Lite on STM32H7) — [arXiv 2411.05873](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05873v1)
- Fixed-point forward gradients for on-device adaptation in vision/audio — [Stepping Forward on the Last Mile, arXiv 2411.04036](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04036v1) **[S]**; tensor-compressed BP-free training names FPGA as a target platform but is not an FPGA implementation — [arXiv 2308.09858](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09858v2.pdf) **[S]**

**Direct feedback alignment and three-factor local rules (mostly spiking)**
- Spike-train-level DFA (Lee et al., Frontiers in Neuroscience 2020): on-chip SNN training on Xilinx ZC706; 96.27% MNIST at 4x reduced input resolution, 76.7% reduction in hardware resources and 31.6% reduction in backward training latency versus a BP-style baseline — [PMC7082320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7082320) **[S]**. Spiker-LL's comparison table lists the same design as 196-50-10, 94.34% MNIST (14x14), 100 MHz, 113 mW, 3.998 ms latency **[2nd]** (accuracy figures conflict between the two sources) — [Spiker-LL](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18003v1)
- Spiker-LL (Politecnico di Torino, May 2026): three-factor rule "STSF" = same-timestep STDP coincidence (an AND gate, no traces) gated by a ternary output error routed through a sparse DFA-style fixed random feedback matrix (one non-zero per hidden neuron); updates every K = 5 timesteps. Pynq-Z2, 16-bit fixed point (8 fractional bits), no DSPs. MNIST 784-200-10: 92% (90% at 8-bit), 13,312 LUT / 6,144 FF / 92 BRAM, 90 MHz, 0.121 ms per image including learning, 509 mW (vector-less estimate). Overhead versus inference-only Spiker: +22% LUTs (10,916 to 13,337), +56 RAMB36 (35 to 91, ROM to dual-port RAM), +16% latency (0.104 to 0.121 ms), +6% power. F-MNIST 81%, DIGITS 93% at 7.7 µs per image. Open source — [Spiker-LL arXiv 2605.18003](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18003v1); [github.com/smilies-polito/Spiker](https://github.com/smilies-polito/Spiker)
- Other on-chip-learning SNN FPGAs listed in Spiker-LL's table **[2nd]**: MorphBungee-Tiny (2025, Zynq-7010, integer e-prop, 256-256-10, 97.55% on scaled MNIST, 100 MHz, 139 mW, 0.25 ms); an "edge processor" (2025, Zynq-7045, aggregate-label rule, 97.21% MNIST, 398 mW, 0.467 ms); SC-PLR (2024, Zynq-7045, 90.19%, 637 mW); TripleBrain (2022, Zynq-7045, 95.10%, 938 mW, 0.74 ms); HaSiST (2023, Virtex-6, 99% DIGITS, 50 MHz) — [Spiker-LL Table I](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18003v1). MorphBungee-Tiny is published in IEEE TCAS-II (DOI 10.1109/TCSII.2025.3623997) — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FTCSII.2025.3623997) **[S]**
- Multiplication-free spike-time learning (Mirsadeghi et al., Apr 2026): single-spike temporal coding, signed backward spikes; Artix-7; 12-bit weights, 10-bit deltas; forward Fmax 125.5 MHz, backward 105.3 MHz; 64-20-20-10 network: forward 1,256 registers / 3,190 LUTs, backward about 1.33 registers and 5.1 LUTs per synapse, no DSPs; forward 5-16 cycles per layer, backward 3-5 cycles per layer; accuracy 98.5% on 8x8 Digits, 97.4% MNIST (784-400-10, software), 84.8% F-MNIST; backward-pass LUTs about 7.4x lower than HaSiST. No repo; power not reported; paper's throughput figures are internally inconsistent — [arXiv 2604.23218](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23218)

**e-prop and recurrent SNNs**
- Restricted e-prop on Stratix V GX (Tianjin Univ., Frontiers 2023): 23-neuron recurrent SNN (LIF + ALIF), 24-bit fixed point; whole system 121,855 LUTs / 18,081 registers / 5.183 W; presynaptic-spike-driven eligibility-trace module cuts LUTs 38.9% and registers 49.5% versus shift-register traces; MNIST subsets 0-4 / 5-9: 88.7% / 84.4% — [Frontiers fnins.2023.1107089](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2023.1107089/full)
- ReckOn (Frenkel and Indiveri; open-source RSNN with modified e-prop, 8-bit weights) ported to Zynq UltraScale+ XCZU5EG (Pynq-ZU): 24-200-2 network for adaptive robotic-arm control; ReckOn uses 30% of LUTs and 30% of BRAM; 83.3% test accuracy on the arm task, 98% on delayed cue accumulation; peak 3.8M input events/s — [arXiv 2405.12849](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12849v2); [github.com/ChFrenkel/ReckOn](https://github.com/ChFrenkel/ReckOn)

**STDP, gradient-free multilayer SNNs, brain-like local rules**
- NeuroCoreX (2025): VHDL SNN emulator on Artix-7 with pair-based rectangular-window STDP, 100 time-multiplexed LIF neurons, 8-bit weights, 305 mW; 68% on DIGITS with STDP disabled at inference; with STDP enabled the hardware weight evolution diverged from simulation; authors note "most FPGA-based implementations still rely heavily on offline training" — [arXiv 2506.14138](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14138)
- ODESA on FPGA (Mehrabi et al., 2023): multi-layer, gradient-free, online-trainable SNN with a per-layer training module, winner-takes-all and adaptive thresholds, all communication by binary spikes — [arXiv 2305.19468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19468) **[S]**
- BCPNN (KTH, 2025): first embedded FPGA accelerator of a brain-like network with local (Hebbian-Bayesian) online learning on Zynq UltraScale+ via HLS; up to 17.5x lower latency and 94% energy saving versus ARM — [arXiv 2506.18530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18530) **[S]**
- An on-chip SNN engine paper argues "SNN backpropagation yields far higher accuracy than STDP even with a very small network size" — [Sci. Reports 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-32120-7.pdf) **[S]**

**Layer-local losses**
- LocalTrain (Oct 2026) is the clearest FPGA example of layer-wise local losses: 76.57% CIFAR-10 versus 79.87% FP32 end-to-end BP (-3.3 points) and 91.85% versus 92.38% on SVHN, at 4.20 W on KV260 — [LocalTrain](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17099v1)

**Forward-Forward**
- Only FPGA implementation found: TU Delft bachelor thesis (Hu and Adriaanse, 2023, mentors C. Frenkel and J. Dauwels), a simplified quantized Forward-Forward on a Digilent ZedBoard reaching 90.5% MNIST and 74.2% Fashion-MNIST; the abstract describes the results as low but promising — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:92d6de17-392c-450a-9af4-4156a93fb445) **[S]**
- Binary-stochastic Forward-Forward (2025) is software-only with estimated hardware savings — [arXiv 2507.06461](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06461v1) **[S]**; Self-Contrastive Forward-Forward targets in-memory crossbars and notes FF "has plateaued" on unsupervised and time-series tasks — [arXiv 2409.11593](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11593v2) **[S]**

**Equilibrium propagation / target propagation**
- EP digital-hardware work found is a McGill thesis and a related 2020 IEEE paper studying quantization and pruning for a digital implementation; other EP hardware is analog/memristive/oscillator/optical — [McGill thesis](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/6682x827f?locale=en) **[S]**; [IEEE 9180548](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9180548) **[S]**

### Inferences
- Among BP-free options, weight/node perturbation is the only one demonstrated for a small dense MLP on FPGA with a favourable hardware argument (re-uses the inference pipeline, tolerates lower precision than BP), but its update time scales with parameter count (one parameter per cycle), so it suits models of roughly 10^2-10^3 parameters.
- Spiking local rules give the lowest overhead (+22% LUTs, no DSPs) but at 88-97.5% MNIST they give up several accuracy points and require spike encoding; they are a reasonable fit only if the signal front end is already event-like.
- For zeroth-order methods, PeZO's result implies perturbation RNG must be designed in (LFSR pools, power-of-two scaling); naive Rademacher/uniform perturbations are cheap but were unstable in their (LLM fine-tuning) setting, whereas the MCU work used Rademacher successfully for small CNNs. The evidence is task-dependent.

### Gaps
- No FPGA implementation of equilibrium propagation, (difference) target propagation, SPSA, or forward-gradient training was found; these remain algorithm-level for FPGA as of Nov 2026.
- No non-spiking DFA FPGA accelerator with a quantitative DFA-versus-BP resource table was found (DFA processors by Han et al. are ASICs and appeared only as references).
- TU Delft Forward-Forward thesis details (whether learning ran fully in fabric, resources) were not verified.
- ODESA, BCPNN and MorphBungee-Tiny full papers were not read.

## 3. Lightweight online learners on FPGA

### Takeaway
The most mature lightweight online learners on FPGA are (1) OS-ELM/ONLAD (rank-1 RLS update of a random-feature autoencoder, about 1 ms or less per update on a Zynq-7020 in 32-bit fixed point, with a forgetting factor for drift), (2) Tsetlin-machine and Tsetlin-WiSARD learners (LUT/LUTRAM-only, 6-100 µs per training sample, 88-98% MNIST), (3) classical LMS filters (64-tap complex LMS weight update in 58.6 ns at 307 MHz on RFSoC), and since 2026 (4) fixed-point KAN SGD at 50-140 ns per update. HDC, prototype/few-shot heads and LoRA-style adapters have FPGA implementations but at 0.1-400 ms scales; FPGA reservoirs with on-chip RLS readout training remain rare.

### Cited Findings

**OS-ELM and the ONLAD line (Keio, Matsutani group)**
- ONLAD Core (Tsukada, Kondo, Matsutani; arXiv 1907.10147, journal venue not verified here): OS-ELM + autoencoder semi-supervised anomaly detector with a forgetting mechanism; batch size fixed to 1 so the matrix inversion becomes a scalar division (1 + h P h^T). Vivado HLS 2018.3 on PYNQ-Z1 (XC7Z020), 100 MHz, 32-bit fixed point. Pre-synthesis utilization is about 40% DSP (of 220), about 30% LUT (of 53,200), about 16% FF, and BRAM from 10% (128 inputs, 16 hidden) to 58.6% (512 inputs, 64 hidden); 1,024 inputs x 64 hidden exceeds BRAM. Post-synthesis: BRAM 55.4%, DSP 32.7%, FF 11.6%, LUT 25.8%. Training and prediction latency each about 1 ms or less; training 1.95-4.51x and prediction 2.29-4.73x faster than BP autoencoders in software; board power 3.1 W, 5.0-25.4x lower than the software baselines. AUC (offline): 0.944 MNIST vs 0.958-0.961 for BP autoencoders. Under concept drift, ONLAD AUC 0.869-0.899 (image sets) versus 0.575-0.591 with forgetting disabled and 0.685-0.787 for BP autoencoders; its forgetting mechanism costs O(N^2) versus O(N^3) for FP-ELM — [ONLAD arXiv 1907.10147](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.10147)
- Overflow/underflow-free bit-width method for OS-ELM circuits (Tsukada and Matsutani, IEICE Trans. 2022): static interval analysis with affine arithmetic, proves the single division's denominator never reaches zero; guarantees no overflow at 1.0-1.5x the area of simulation-tuned bit widths. Motivation given: in on-chip learning the ranges of intermediate values keep changing as training continues — [arXiv 2103.09791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09791) **[S]**
- ODL deployed on a Raspberry Pi Pico (Sunaga, Kondo, Matsutani; arXiv 2203.01077 v4): four OS-ELM instances (256-32-256), 176 KiB float32, 2 KiB input-data memory versus 600-2400 KiB for BP baselines; retraining triggered by a field engineer's button or automatically by a sequential concept-drift detector; data at [github.com/matutani/cooling-fan](https://github.com/matutani/cooling-fan) — [arXiv 2203.01077](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.01077v4)
- Federated OS-ELM (Ito, Tsukada, Matsutani; IEEE Access 2021): devices train OS-ELM locally and merge intermediate results (U, V matrices via a modified E2LM) rather than gradients; software only, no FPGA — [arXiv 2002.12301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12301) **[S]**
- OSOS-ELM (Zang, Li, Li; Strathclyde 2025): OS-ELM with one-sided Jacobi SVD for the initial solve, on ZCU104 (HLS 2018.3), 64-bit floating point; L = 150 hidden nodes, 256 inputs: training + inference use 75,701 + 22,381 LUT, 59 + 71 DSP, 161 + 145 BRAM (98% of BRAM); 1.05 ms per training sample and 0.18 ms per inference; small config (16 inputs, L = 50): 0.11 ms training, 0.018 ms inference; 4.6 W. A Jetson Xavier NX GPU was faster at L = 150; the authors find the FPGA better suited to small networks — [arXiv 2504.09028](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09028v1)
- Counter-evidence for OS-ELM under drift: in the KAN online-learning paper's drifting qubit-readout benchmark a float OS-ELM with 10 hidden features scored 41.8% versus 92.8% for the fixed-point KAN and about 50% for quantized MLPs — [arXiv 2602.02056](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02056v4)

**LoRA-style and last-layer updates**
- Skip2-LoRA (Matsutani et al., ASP-DAC 2025): LoRA adapters that skip to the last layer so base-model forward results can be cached; fine-tuning time reduced 90.0% on average on a $15 single-board computer; an FPGA demo reports on-device fine-tuning in 0.34 s — [arXiv 2410.21073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21073) **[S]**; [Keio demo page](https://www.arc.ics.keio.ac.jp/?p=4361) **[S]**
- InstantFT (Sugiura and Matsutani, 2025): Kria KV260, PL at 200 MHz; five rank-4 LoRA adapters on a LeNet-5-like CNN feeding the last layer in parallel; Q8.16 activations and Q4.12 parameters/gradients; forward cache quantized to 4-bit NF4 (7.2x smaller); 10-epoch fine-tune of the adapters in 0.36 s (17.4x faster than the CPU counterpart), 4.03 W, 16.3x energy efficiency; accuracy within 3.2% of software; 91.8% on RotMNIST after a 90-degree drift. LUT/DSP numbers only in a figure; no repo — [arXiv 2506.06505](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06505v1)
- BOOST transfer-learning mode (last 2-6 conv layers + FC trained on chip) is the CNN equivalent: about a quarter to half of full backprop time — [BOOST](https://phwl.github.io/assets/papers/boost_iccad23.pdf)

**Tsetlin machines**
- Prescott et al. 2023: modular FPGA architecture for offline + online TM training on a Zybo Z7-20; inference and feedback for all clauses in two clock cycles ("one datapoint per clock" throughput); demonstrates learning from limited initial data (+12% accuracy over 16 online iterations on Iris), adding a previously unseen class at runtime (classes over-provisioned at synthesis), and recovering from 20% stuck-at-0 Tsetlin automata by continued online learning. Online data arrive through a cyclic RAM buffer; accuracy checks can enable/disable online learning or trigger full on-chip retraining. Labels are assumed to come from user feedback, a central system, or confident self-classification; unlabelled operation is future work. Resources and clock not reported; 1.725 W estimated (1.4 W is the microcontroller) — [arXiv 2306.01027](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.01027)
- Dynamic Tsetlin Machine (DTM; IEEE TCAS-I 2025): run-time reconfigurable on-chip training that switches dataset, model architecture and size without resynthesis. Two configurations: PYNQ-Z1 (XC7Z020, 50 MHz) and ZCU104 (XCZU7EV, 100 MHz). Reported per-sample latencies 50-99 µs training and 22-45 µs inference; MNIST accuracy 95.1% (small) and 97.74% (large); a 2000-clause Coalesced-TM configuration uses 104,222 LUT / 59,610 FF / 25 DSP / 37 BRAM / 96 URAM and reaches 18,281 training and 42,878 inference datapoints/s; claims 2.54x more GOP/s/W and 6x less power than the next-best comparable trainer. The column-to-configuration mapping in my extraction was ambiguous; the same group's later table lists the XC7Z020 DTM as 43,497 LUT / 33,256 FF / 138 BRAM / 6 DSP, 95.1 µs training, 44.7 µs inference, 4.36 W, 95.10% MNIST — [DTM arXiv 2504.19797](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19797v1); [TsetlinWiSARD Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24186)

**Weightless / LUT-based networks with online learning**
- TsetlinWiSARD (Duan, Sartori, Shafik, Yakovlev; DAC 2026): one Tsetlin automaton per WiSARD LUT entry; on a wrong prediction the addressed entries are incremented in the correct class's discriminator and decremented in the wrongly predicted one, each with a feedback probability from a bitwise LFSR; inference structure is unchanged WiSARD. LUT contents and automaton states live in LUTRAM (distributed RAM), so the model's "weights" are runtime-writable LUT contents. XC7Z020 (PYNQ-Z1), 150 six-input LUTs per class x 10 classes, 32 states: 33,596 LUTs (75.4% used as memory) / 25,927 FFs / 2 BRAM / 1 DSP (PS interface only); 6.4 µs per sample for training and inference including data transfer **[PDF-extract]**; 1.56 W dynamic, core logic about 10% of it; on-chip accuracy after 100 epochs 88.02% MNIST / 79.41% F-MNIST / 72.06% K-MNIST (simulated 300-LUT version 92.1% MNIST). On-chip accuracy is below off-chip because short-period LFSR imbalance biases feedback. Claims 22% fewer resources, 93.3% lower latency and 64.2% lower power than other FPGA training accelerators, and over 1000x faster than the prior WiSARD trainer (hashed WNN on XC7Z020: 8.3 ms training with an ARM core in the loop). Open source — [arXiv 2603.24186](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24186); [github.com/nsd5g13/TsetlinWiSARD](https://github.com/nsd5g13/TsetlinWiSARD)
- Inference-only weightless baselines for scale **[2nd]**: LogicWiSARD 560 ns, 91.0% MNIST; BTHOWeN 15,756 LUT / 3,522 FF, 250 ns, 0.30 W, 93.4% MNIST — [TsetlinWiSARD Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24186)

**Kolmogorov-Arnold networks**
- Only online-learning KAN on FPGA found is Hoang et al. 2026 (see Section 1): 50-140 ns per full update on XCVU13P, 430-10,592 LUTs; KAN activations are bounded by the min/max learned coefficients and coefficient gradients are bounded by the upstream gradient because B-spline values lie in [0,1]; in a drifting-regression test the KAN stayed stable across bit widths while a larger MLP showed a "sharp precision cliff"; a frozen learner fell to about 20% under drift on rotating Digits where the online learner held about 80% — [arXiv 2602.02056](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02056v4)

**Classical adaptive filters (baseline)**
- 64-tap complex LMS FIR as a custom QICK readout module on RFSoC ZCU216 (XCZU49DR), 307.2 MHz data clock (Johnson et al., arXiv Oct 2026): LMS weight update takes 18 clocks = 58.6 ns (streaming); filtered path 86 clocks = 280 ns of which vendor FIR cores are 79 clocks; per-pulse coefficient reload 128 clocks = 417 ns; whole design 52,244 LUT / 47,664 FF / 1,558 DSP48E2 (512 for the parallel LMS update) / 16.5 BRAM; coefficients in Q27.16. ### Inferences
- Update-latency ladder (per sample, from the cited works): KAN SGD 50-140 ns (VU13P); LMS 64-tap 58.6 ns (RFSoC); WP/BP small MLP 1.3-2.5 µs (Kintex-7); sparse MLP BP 2.27 µs (Artix-7 at 15 MHz); TsetlinWiSARD 6.4 µs; Conv-TM 27 µs; DTM 50-99 µs; Spiker-LL 121 µs; ONLAD and OSOS-ELM 0.1-1 ms (HLS, sequential matrix ops); ImageHD 0.48 ms; InstantFT 0.36 s per fine-tune job. The millisecond figures for OS-ELM reflect sequential HLS matrix code on Zynq-7000 and 64-bit float respectively, not a fundamental limit.
- OS-ELM's rank-1 update is an RLS readout on a fixed random hidden layer, so "ESN/random features + RLS" and "OS-ELM" are the same hardware problem; the published FPGA record for it is ONLAD-class designs, and published FPGA reservoirs mostly do not train on chip.
- Tsetlin-automaton learners are the only family here whose learning hardware is pure increment/decrement/compare in LUTs/LUTRAM with no multipliers, and which demonstrated runtime class addition and self-repair; their weakness is Boolean inputs (thresholded features) and accuracy below CNNs.
- The LMS qubit-readout result is a caution for faint-signal detection: a linear adaptive front end feeding an integrate-and-threshold decision may add no discrimination power, so nonlinear learners (KAN/MLP/TM) or matched-filter weighting are where gains would come from.

- No FPGA implementation of an ESN with an RLS-trained readout with resource numbers was verified; Kalman-filter-trained networks on FPGA were not searched in depth.
- ONLAD source code location was not confirmed; successors (ODL core ASIC, ODLHAR, OS-ELM Q-networks on FPGA) were not verified in this pass.
- HDC online-learning FPGA resource/latency numbers other than ImageHD are abstract-level only.
- Skip2-LoRA FPGA accelerator details (device, resources) come only from a demo page; PEFSL numbers are abstract-level.
- No hardware LoRA-on-MLP update path for sub-microsecond models was found; published LoRA FPGA work is CNN fine-tuning at 0.3-0.4 s per job.

## 4. Hybrid schemes: host trains, FPGA infers and hot-swaps weights

### Takeaway
Three weight-update mechanisms are documented: memory-mapped (AXI-Lite) writes into weight registers/RAM (SLAC SNL, FINN's runtime-writable weights), double-buffered "ping-pong" parameter memories with a mux toggle at the end of a training step (Sydney weight-perturbation design), and dynamic partial reconfiguration for structure changes (0.11-0.17 s on an Alveo U200). The best end-to-end example of a host-in-the-loop learner is KIT's KINGFISHER reinforcement-learning controller (2.8 µs inference loop on a Versal, PPO trained on a control-room PC, new weights loaded at run time after every 2048-step episode), but almost no paper reports the weight-swap latency itself.

### Cited Findings
- hls4ml default: "hls4ml embeds all model parameters into the PL during synthesis, meaning any retraining or parameter update requires regenerating and reloading a new bitstream" — [SNL/Auto-SNL paper, arXiv 2508.21739](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21739v1). A 2023 paper states "neither FINN nor HLS4ML ever proposed a reconfigurable solution for runtime adaptive environments" — [arXiv 2309.13321](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.13321) **[S]**
- SLAC Neural Network Library (SNL): HLS inference framework whose defining feature is dynamic reload of weights and biases without resynthesis; "Weights and biases are loaded via AXI-Lite registers, while AXI-Stream facilitates real-time input and output data flow"; managed from SLAC's Rogue software. Benchmarked on ZCU102 at 10 ns clock against hls4ml: SNL had lower latency in three of four benchmark models, uses fewer LUTs for fully connected networks but "consistently uses more BRAM and mostly higher FF counts". Reload time is not reported and non-interruption of inference is asserted, not measured — [arXiv 2508.21739](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21739v1); [github.com/slaclab/rogue](https://github.com/slaclab/rogue). A DIII-D plasma diagnostic deployment on KCU1500 reports 4.4 µs network latency and hardware-verified reload — [arXiv 2511.21924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21924) **[S]**
- FINN: MVAU layers have a node attribute `runtime_writeable_weights` ("(mem_mode = internal_decoupled only) whether weights will be writeable through an AXI-lite interface during runtime"), with the caveat: "After using AXI lite to either read or write the weights, always 'flush' the accelerator by first passing a dummy input vector through the accelerator. This will get rid of any old weight data from the weight FIFOs." `mem_mode = internal_embedded` bakes weights into logic ("long compile/synth times"); `external` streams weights from an external streamer — [FINN source, matrixvectoractivation.py](https://github.com/Xilinx/finn/blob/main/src/finn/custom_op/fpgadataflow/matrixvectoractivation.py). A user report notes the Zynq build flow did not expose the per-layer AXI weight interfaces without this option — [FINN discussion #380](https://github.com/Xilinx/finn/discussions/380) **[S]**
- Ping-pong (double-buffered) parameters: "We use ping-pong buffering for the parameters, such that the training core and the prediction core can operate side-by-side. When a new set of parameters are ready after a training epoch," the controller toggles the multiplexer/demultiplexer select lines so inference switches to the new set while training writes the other — [Siddhartha et al., FPT'18](https://phwl.github.io/assets/papers/wp_fpt18.pdf)
- KINGFISHER (KIT, online RL at the KARA storage ring): Versal VCK190; actor network (8 inputs, 16-neuron ReLU hidden layer, linear output; best agent used 12 neurons by zeroing weights) runs in the AI Engine array; FPGA logic does data forwarding, exploration-noise generation (sum of 16 uniform randoms, run-time selectable sigma) and DMA of observation/action streams to DDR (the "experience accumulator") fed by a 40 Gbps Aurora link. "The whole inference loop has a latency of 2.8 µs" at a 2.7 MHz sampling rate; episodes are 2048 steps (784 µs). PPO (modified Stable-Baselines3) trains on a control-room computer; new parameters are "loaded onto the AIE kernel at run-time" after each episode and the network structure can change without re-implementing firmware. 100 training steps took 260 s of CPU time against 0.076 s of machine interaction time. Weight-transfer time is not stated; code on request only — [arXiv 2409.16177](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16177v1)
- Masked partial updates with DPR (Kang and Park, Sensors 2025): a server-side Alveo U200 (300 MHz kernel, Vitis 2023.1) does importance analysis and layer-wise partial retraining and sends only masked deltas (about 27-28% of full-model payload) to an edge device (a Cortex-M0 MCU) that forms W_eff = W_base + M * dW; the paper describes writing masked updates to a shadow buffer and activating them by "a fast pointer swap rather than reloading the entire model" after integrity checks, as recommended practice rather than a measured design. DPR latency 0.11-0.17 s depending on module size; steady-state adaptation cycle about 0.65 s; accuracy within 1.0-1.2% of full retraining — [PMC12736569](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12736569/)
- UART/host-loaded weights with on-chip adaptation and readback: NeuroCoreX loads weights and learning-rule parameters from a PC over 1 Mbps UART before execution and allows reading the adapted weights back — [arXiv 2506.14138](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14138)
- Run-time reconfiguration of the model itself without resynthesis: DTM switches dataset, architecture and model size at run time by reprogramming clause/weight matrices — [arXiv 2504.19797](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19797v1)
- LUTRAM as the weight store: TsetlinWiSARD notes modern FPGA LUTs "function as distributed RAMs" (LUTRAM/MLAB) that are "dynamically writable memory blocks at runtime", giving LUT-network designs a write path that avoids partial reconfiguration — [arXiv 2603.24186](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24186)
- Hardware forward / software backward split: a TU Delft 2025 thesis runs a sparse FC SNN accelerator's forward pass in fabric on a Kria KV260 with backpropagation in software on the SoC CPU, with compressed latent replay — [TU Delft record](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:37fde655-4ce0-41e2-a415-9908c6c9aeeb) **[S]**. Fox et al. similarly partition GEMM to PL and the rest to ARM — [FPT'19](https://phwl.github.io/assets/papers/lptrain_fpt19.pdf)
- Remote weight update of an hls4ml IP core on an Arria 10 SoC (Crossfield Technology / Fermilab SBIR, 2022 workshop abstract) — [CERN Indico contribution](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1156222/contributions/5062809/contribution.pdf) **[S]** (not verified beyond the search snippet)
- Federated/edge loop: OS-ELM federated learning merges per-device intermediate matrices; evaluated in software — [arXiv 2002.12301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12301) **[S]**

### Inferences
- For a model with weights in registers/LUTRAM/BRAM on an Alveo card, the update path would be the XDMA/XRT AXI-Lite control interface or an AXI-Stream weight channel; the published designs show the pattern (shadow copy, write, flush or toggle) but not PCIe-level timing. FINN's "flush with a dummy input" caveat shows that streamed-weight (decoupled) architectures have stale weights in FIFOs, so an atomic swap needs either a ping-pong memory with a select bit or an explicit quiesce.
- The KINGFISHER split (hard-real-time inference path + DMA experience capture to bulk memory + asynchronous host training + per-episode weight reload) maps directly onto a U50: fabric model, HBM as experience/replay store, host training over PCIe.
- Partial reconfiguration at 0.1-0.2 s per swap is two to five orders of magnitude slower than memory-mapped weight writes and is only justified when the topology (not just weights) changes or when weights are folded into LUT logic (hls4ml/LogicNets-style designs).

### Gaps
- No paper was found that reports measured weight hot-swap latency (ns-µs) or maximum update rate for AXI-Lite or streamed weight updates over PCIe/Ethernet on an Alveo-class card.
- No published design was found that uses DPR specifically to update weights baked into LUTs of a LUT-based network (LogicNets/PolyLUT-style) at run time with timing numbers.
- No FPGA-in-the-loop federated learning system with measured results was found.
- SNL's source repository and its register map/reload protocol were not located in the paper.

## 5. Stability concerns for always-on learning in fixed point

### Takeaway
Documented fixed-point failure modes are: sub-LSB updates truncating to zero (weights stall or decay to all-zero without any flag), saturation/overflow of low-precision accumulators and softmax, shifting value ranges in recursive (RLS/OS-ELM) updates, and LFSR-induced bias; documented mitigations are forgetting factors, saturating arithmetic with convergent rounding, shift-based step-size schedules, stochastic/pseudo-stochastic rounding, weight averaging, statically proven bit widths, accuracy-gated learning, and small on-chip plus large off-chip replay buffers. No work was found that uses FPGA HBM as a replay buffer, and shadow-model/rollback safeguards appear only as recommended practice, not as evaluated designs.

### Cited Findings

**Quantization stall, saturation and overflow**
- LMS on RFSoC (paraphrased from the paper's description, not verbatim): coefficient updates are truncated toward zero at the coefficient LSB, so updates smaller than one LSB vanish, and a filter operating below that amplitude floor can train to all-zero coefficients without any status indicator flagging it; the design uses Q27.16 coefficients and a power-normalized step; no clipping/saturation handling is described — [arXiv 2610.00783](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00783v3)
- NITI: naive integer SGD updates often overflowed or underflowed, leaving most values saturated at ±127 or 0 (paraphrase); fixed by shifting int32 results right by max(0, b-7) using the effective bit width b, and gradients by max(0, b-m_u); the update bit width m_u (5 then 4 then 3) is the learning-rate schedule. Pseudo-stochastic rounding (90.7%) matches true stochastic rounding (90.8%) and beats round-to-nearest (89.5%) on CIFAR-10 while using 25.3% fewer LUTs and 63.0% fewer FFs than an RNG-based rounder — [NITI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.13108)
- Fixed-point BP versus WP: at Q2.8 BP reached only 52% on Iris and failed entirely on the RF autoencoder while WP still trained (unstably); softmax was removed because of fixed-point under/overflow; the paper stresses managing integer versus fractional bits when designing the network — [Siddhartha et al., FPT'18](https://phwl.github.io/assets/papers/wp_fpt18.pdf)
- Block floating point: exponents are recomputed every iteration and "should be set to the largest exponent in a block to avoid overflow"; stochastic rounding gives zero expected rounding error; stochastic weight averaging with a higher learning rate recovers quantization error with all-8-bit values — [Fox et al., FPT'19](https://phwl.github.io/assets/papers/lptrain_fpt19.pdf)
- OS-ELM recursive update: manually tuned bit widths "may cause overflow or underflow which can lead to unexpected behavior of the circuit"; static affine-arithmetic analysis yields overflow-free widths at 1.0-1.5x area; ONLAD itself used 32-bit fixed point — [arXiv 2103.09791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09791) **[S]**; [ONLAD](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.10147)
- OSOS-ELM found regression error only converged above 20 fractional bits for one task and resorted to 64-bit floating point in hardware — [arXiv 2504.09028](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09028v1)
- KAN versus MLP under fixed-point online SGD: KAN updates use ap_fixed with AP_RND_CONV and AP_SAT; propositions bound KAN activations by the coefficient range and gradients by the upstream gradient; quantized MLPs collapse under drift in the qubit-readout benchmark while the quantized KAN tracks it; LayerNorm and LSUV initialization did not close the MLP gap; learning rates are compile-time constants (0.5, 0.05, 1e-3 for the three KAN tasks) — [arXiv 2602.02056](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02056v4)
- LocalTrain keeps INT16 "master" weights for the final classifier and a 48-bit gradient accumulator while computing in INT8 — [LocalTrain](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17099v1)
- PRNG quality: TsetlinWiSARD's on-chip accuracy fell below software because "short-period imbalances" in LFSR bits gave some automata more feedback than others; PeZO needed pool sizes/RNG counts of 2^n - 1 and rotation to avoid correlated perturbations — [TsetlinWiSARD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24186); [PeZO](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20314v2)
- Hardware/simulation divergence when plasticity is on: NeuroCoreX's STDP-enabled weight evolution diverged from the floating-point simulator and the authors say further tuning is needed — [arXiv 2506.14138](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14138)

**Drift, forgetting and when to learn**
- ONLAD's forgetting factor is what makes always-on learning track drift: AUC 0.869-0.899 with forgetting versus 0.575-0.591 without on image datasets with concept drift; a semi-supervised detector also needs care not to learn anomalies as normal (its training is on data assumed normal) — [ONLAD](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.10147)
- ODL practice: retraining is gated, either by an operator pressing a train button to mark the current input as the new normal, or automatically when a sequential concept-drift detector fires; drift types named are sudden, gradual, incremental and reoccurring — [arXiv 2203.01077](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.01077v4)
- Tsetlin online architecture: periodic on-chip accuracy analysis on a held validation set can enable/disable online learning, adjust sensitivity, or trigger full on-chip retraining below a threshold; over-provisioned clauses/classes can be enabled later — [arXiv 2306.01027](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.01027)
- Frozen versus online under drift: about 20% versus about 80% accuracy on slowly rotating Digits — [arXiv 2602.02056](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02056v4)
- 2026 survey of about 70 TinyML on-device-learning works organizes the field by type of post-deployment distribution change and notes a gap between method benchmarks and real deployment scenarios — [arXiv 2605.31226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31226) **[S]**

**Replay buffers and memory hierarchy**
- Chameleon (NUS, DATE 2023): dual-memory replay for online continual learning with a short-term store in on-chip memory (0.3 MB) and a long-term store off-chip, with user-aware and class-prototype-based sampling; on a ZCU102 the reported latency/energy are 413 ms / 1.22 J versus 2,788 ms / 8.62 J for the Latent Replay baseline (about 7x) **[PDF-extract; column mapping inferred]**; EWC++ and LwF performed poorly due to gradient explosion on earlier domains — [Chameleon, DATE 2023](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~tulika/DATE_2023.pdf)
- Compressed latent replay for SNNs (TU Delft thesis 2025): autoencoder plus spike-count compression cut MNIST replay storage from 245 KB to 62.81 KB with little accuracy loss — [TU Delft record](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:37fde655-4ce0-41e2-a415-9908c6c9aeeb) **[S]**
- Replay-free bounded memory: ImageHD keeps only a capped set of cluster hypervectors (worst-case 126.6 KB) with merge-on-overflow — [arXiv 2604.21280](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21280v1)
- HBM in training accelerators has been used for training data/activations/weights, not replay: Stratix 10 MX HBM2 trainer (ICCAD'20) and NITI's U280 prototype ("data passed through HBM") — [paper record](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10214138) **[S]**; [NITI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.13108). KINGFISHER stores experience streams in DDR via DMA for the host trainer — [arXiv 2409.16177](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16177v1)
- TinyCL continual-learning accelerator is a 65 nm ASIC, not FPGA — [arXiv 2402.09780](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09780v3) **[S]**

**Safeguards**
- Shadow buffer + atomic pointer swap after integrity checks is described as practice for partial updates — [Kang and Park 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12736569/); ping-pong parameter buffers let training never write the live copy — [Siddhartha et al., FPT'18](https://phwl.github.io/assets/papers/wp_fpt18.pdf); KINGFISHER can switch off neurons by zeroing weights and select exploration-noise sigma at run time — [arXiv 2409.16177](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16177v1)

### Inferences
- For "faint signals previously missed", the sub-LSB stall is the most directly relevant hazard: small error-driven updates from weak events are exactly the ones a truncating fixed-point update discards. The cited mitigations are wider weight stores than compute operands (INT16/Q27.16/48-bit accumulators), stochastic or pseudo-stochastic rounding of the update, or accumulate-then-apply (NITI/LocalTrain-style gradient accumulators).
- A forgetting factor fixes drift tracking but is itself a forgetting mechanism: an always-on semi-supervised learner will also adapt to a persistent weak signal and stop flagging it. Gating learning (drift detector, confidence, operator/host label) is the published countermeasure; none of the FPGA works evaluates the "learn the rare positive without absorbing it into normal" case.
- An 8 GB HBM replay/experience store is an open design space: published FPGA replay designs budget 0.06-0.3 MB on chip, and the only large on-card experience capture found (KINGFISHER) serves a host trainer.

### Gaps
- No FPGA paper was found that evaluates long-horizon (days+) fixed-point online learning for weight drift or saturation statistics.
- No evaluated FPGA implementation of shadow-model validation with automatic rollback was found; no FPGA work using HBM as a replay buffer was found.
- Hardware learning-rate scheduling beyond NITI's shrinking update bit width and compile-time constants was not found documented for small online learners.
- Chameleon table columns were inferred from extracted text; re-check against the PDF before quoting.

## 6. Most promising and tractable approaches for a single-FPGA research prototype (late 2026), and reusable open-source repositories

### Takeaway
On the evidence collected, the lowest-risk path is a hybrid: a small fixed-point model in fabric with runtime-writable weights and a ping-pong weight memory, experience capture to on-card memory, host-side training, plus an optional in-fabric fast learner restricted to a last layer or local-support parameters (KAN-style SGD, OS-ELM/RLS readout, or Tsetlin/WiSARD automata). The 2026 KAN on-chip SGD result (50-140 ns per update, about 10k LUTs) and TsetlinWiSARD (6.4 µs per sample, open-source RTL flow) are the most directly reusable demonstrations of sub-10 µs in-fabric learning; OS-ELM/ONLAD is the most mature drift-tracking anomaly detector but published implementations are about 1 ms per update.

### Inferences
- Ranking for a single-U50 research prototype, weighing demonstrated latency, resource cost, numerical robustness evidence and reuse (my assessment, not a claim from any one source):
  1. **Hybrid host-trained model with atomic weight swap + HBM experience buffer** (KINGFISHER/SNL pattern, FPT'18 ping-pong memory). Lowest algorithmic risk; lets the host use full-precision training, replay and validation before a swap; the unmeasured piece is PCIe weight-update latency, which the prototype could itself characterize (a publishable gap).
  2. **In-fabric per-sample SGD on a small KAN or last-layer/readout only** (Hoang et al. 2026 pattern). Best published latency (sub-200 ns) and explicit fixed-point stability arguments (bounded activations/gradients, saturating arithmetic); risks: single preprint, benchmarks that appear to be synthetic (defined by drift/noise parameters rather than recorded hardware data), no code released.
  3. **OS-ELM / RLS readout with forgetting factor on a fixed feature extractor** (ONLAD pattern). Most mature drift-tracking anomaly detector; needs a parallel RTL/HLS re-implementation to reach µs-class updates and careful bit-width analysis (affine-arithmetic method) or wide fixed point.
  4. **Tsetlin / Tsetlin-WiSARD learner** for a Boolean-feature detector head. Multiplier-free, LUTRAM-native, open source, µs updates, shown to add classes and self-repair online; risks: thresholded inputs and lower accuracy; needs labels or confident self-labels.
  5. **Weight perturbation** as a drop-in learner for an existing inference pipeline when BP hardware is undesirable; scales poorly beyond about 10^3 parameters.
  6. **Lower priority for this use case:** full integer CNN backprop (large, ms-s per step, about a year of engineering per the Zhao et al. estimate), spiking local rules (unless the front end is event-based), HDC (ms-scale, clustering-quality evidence only), Forward-Forward / equilibrium propagation / zeroth-order (no credible FPGA implementations yet).
- A combined design is consistent with the literature: frozen or slowly host-updated feature extractor + fast in-fabric adaptive head + ping-pong weights + gated learning, with the host acting as the shadow-model validator; each element has a published precedent, but the combination with HBM replay has not been published.
- Every in-fabric learner cited assumes a supervision signal (labels, reconstruction error, reward, or confident self-prediction). For "events previously missed" the realistic signal sources are reconstruction/prediction error (ONLAD-style), delayed host labels, or operator feedback; the choice of signal constrains the algorithm more than FPGA resources do.

### Gaps
- No public code was found for the two most relevant sub-microsecond learners (KAN on-chip SGD; LMS QICK module); ONLAD/OS-ELM FPGA source availability was not confirmed.
- No head-to-head comparison of these learner families on a common faint-signal or streaming-detection benchmark on the same FPGA exists in the sources found; all cross-family numbers above come from different boards, clocks, datasets and measurement boundaries.
- Works dated late 2026 were found only as arXiv preprints (LocalTrain 2610.17099, LMS readout 2610.00783, an FPGA SNN for spatiotemporal pattern learning 2610.17463 not read); peer-review status is unknown, and FPGA'27/FCCM'27-cycle papers are not yet visible.
- Tool limitations: several primary PDFs could only be read through text extraction, and some sources were only seen as search snippets (tagged [S]); those numbers should be re-verified before publication.
