# Faint-signal (low-SNR) detection with ML, recovery of missed events, and online adaptation: methods, evidence, evaluation practice, datasets

Notes compiled 2026-11-21. Conventions used below:
- **[real]** = result on real detector/instrument data; **[sim]** = simulated noise or fully synthetic data; **[semi]** = synthetic signals injected into real noise.
- **[snippet-only]** = the number came from a search-engine summary of the source, not from the fetched primary text. Treat as needing a check before it goes in a report.
- **[abstract-only]** = only the abstract was readable; details in the body were not verified.

## 1. Evidence by domain: does ML find events that classical pipelines missed, and where does it match, beat, or fall short of the matched filter?

### Takeaway
Across domains the pattern is consistent: learned detectors reach roughly 91-95% of matched-filter sensitive distance in stationary Gaussian noise and only win outright where the classical method is sub-optimal (unknown or un-templated waveforms, non-Gaussian noise, noise-uncertainty-limited energy detection, or compute-limited template banks). On real data, ML has produced new candidates in gravitational waves, large catalogue expansions in seismology, and new candidates in radio searches, but most "missed event" claims remain unconfirmed by independent pipelines or follow-up, and the best real-noise results come from large ResNets, not tiny models.

### Cited Findings

**Gravitational waves (GW)**
- MLGWSC-1 (the first ML GW search mock data challenge): 6 submissions, 4 of them ML; two detectors (H1, L1) at 2048 Hz; one month (2,592,000 s) of test background per dataset; signals are IMRPhenomXPHM binary black holes with merger times 24-30 s apart. Datasets 1-3 use Gaussian noise of increasing realism (fixed PSD; 20 O3a-derived PSDs; time-varying PSD with precession and higher modes); dataset 4 uses real O3a noise from GWOSC with time shifts. — [MLGWSC-1, ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146)
- MLGWSC-1 sensitive distance at FAR = 1 per month (Mpc): PyCBC 2491.5 / 1543.8 / 1577.2 / 1544.3 on datasets 1-4; best ML on Gaussian noise (TPI FSU Jena CNN) 2362.5 / 1455.1 / 1487.7 but 0.0 on real noise; Virgo-AUTh ResNet 2116.4 / 1242.4 / 1240.7 / 1091.8; MFCNN 779.2 / 448.6 / 355.8 / 649.8; CNN-Coinc 1001.6 / 373.6 / 401.6 / 0.0; cWB (wavelet excess power + XGBoost, unmodelled) 1252.1 on DS3 and 1229.1 on DS4. — [MLGWSC-1, ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146)
- Headline from the abstract: best ML reaches up to 95% of matched-filter sensitive distance in simulated Gaussian noise at 1/month **[sim]** and 70% in real O3a noise **[semi]**; at high FAR (>= 200/month) some ML submissions slightly exceed PyCBC. — [MLGWSC-1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11146)
- The MLGWSC-1 authors attribute the real-noise shortfall to non-Gaussian transients being misclassified (ML searches lacked the excision and signal-consistency checks of classical pipelines), non-stationarity, short input windows that miss long signals, and weak inter-detector consistency checks. Two CNNs produced zero sensitive distance on real noise at 1/month because background triggers dominated. — [MLGWSC-1, ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146)
- PyCBC in MLGWSC-1 ran in a standard configuration, not tuned to the challenge's mass range; its template bank was aligned-spin, dominant-mode only. — [review, arXiv:2412.15046 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15046); [MLGWSC-1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146)
- AresGW (post-challenge update of the Virgo-AUTh entry): 54-layer 1-D ResNet (27 residual blocks), input 2 x 2048 samples (1 s at 2048 Hz, two detectors), Deep Adaptive Input Normalization, SNR curriculum, trained on 12 days of O3a noise with ~38k injected waveforms in ~31 h on one A6000 GPU. The authors report it surpasses the challenge's PyCBC configuration at all tested FARs on dataset 4 **[semi]**, with about +/-3% variance in sensitive distance across test sets at 1/month. Parameter count not reported. — [Nousi et al., arXiv:2211.01520](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.01520)
- AresGW applied to real O3 data: eight new candidates with p_astro > 0.5 (cumulative p_astro 5.94), FARs between 0.27/yr and 49/yr; described as the first new coincident-network candidates found by an ML algorithm. Within its training range (chirp mass 10-40 Msun) it recovers 34 of 43 known catalogue events; outside that range 10 of 55. Background: 10 years of time-shifted O3a noise, with Gravity Spy glitches removed. **[real]** — [Koloniari et al., arXiv:2407.07820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07820)
- Independent ML searches of the same O3 data disagree on new candidates: AresGW reports 52 events (39 GWTC-3, 5 from other catalogues, 8 unique); Aframe reports 41 (38 GWTC-3, 3 other catalogues, 0 unique). Only 10 non-GWTC candidates have been found by more than one independent O3 search. — [hybrid search paper, arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516)
- Aframe (1-D ResNet34 with GroupNorm, 2048 Hz, 1.5 s windows, inference at 4 Hz): larger sensitive volume than all GWTC-3 configurations of MBTA, PyCBC, GstLAL and cWB in the 35-35 Msun bin, comparable in the 35-20 bin, worse at lower masses **[semi]**. Median total latency ~3.1 s (network inference < 10 ms; latency is dominated by 1 s output integration, 1 s resampling crop, 0.5 s whitening crop); in a mock replay median reporting latency 3.9 s versus 12.3 s for matched-filter pipelines. — [Marx et al., arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18661)
- Aframe's full O3 offline search: 100 years of time-slides for background; recovered 38 GWTC-3 candidates with p_astro > 0.5; found no previously unreported candidates; sensitivity similar to matched filtering in the 35-35 Msun bin and decreasing as chirp mass decreases. **[real]** — [arXiv:2505.21261 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21261)
- Aframe ran live in the second half of O4 and is described as the first AI search to detect multiple BBHs live (23 BBH public alerts **[snippet-only]**); a July 2026 extension to binary neutron stars uses heterodyning so the same BBH architecture suffices, and claims sensitivity "comparable to matched-filter pipelines" on one non-flagship GPU. Numerical sensitivity not given in the abstract. **[abstract-only]** — [Gupta et al., arXiv:2607.01372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01372)
- Sage (Nagarajan & Messenger, 2025): on MLGWSC-1 dataset 4 at 1/month it detects 4290 injections versus 3857 for PyCBC (+11.2%) and 2893 for AresGW (+48.3%), with sensitive distance ~6.5% above PyCBC **[semi]**. It still detects fewer low-chirp-mass signals than PyCBC. Architecture: multiscale 1-D convolutional front end + CBAM-augmented ResNet50 with auxiliary regression heads; trained on ~160 days of O3 noise. Tested only on one month (O3a days 1-30). — [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13846)
- Hybrid matched-filter + deep-learning ranking (Dec 2025): a 97,802-template bank produces SNR time series; a network scores the top-10 templates each second and its output is the ranking statistic. Offline O3 search recovered 31 LVK candidates with p_astro >= 0.5, one IAS-only candidate, and one new candidate (p_astro 0.63). Less sensitive than PyCBC-BBH overall, falling off below source-frame chirp mass ~25 Msun and network SNR <~ 15, but it adds to combined sensitive hypervolume above ~20-20 Msun. **[real]** — [arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516)
- Single-detector Gaussian-noise study (Schafer et al.): a 6-conv + 3-dense CNN retains >= 91.5% of matched-filter sensitive distance at all tested FARs down to 1/month (~2150 Mpc, matched filter up to ~200 Mpc farther at low FAR, equal at >= 1e5 false alarms/month). **[sim]** — [arXiv:2106.03741](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2106.03741)
- Matched filtering with a template bank is formally equivalent to a specific neural network; networks initialised as matched filters and then trained (MNet-Shallow, MNet-Deep) "significantly outperform matched filtering at false positive rates above 5e-3 %" on real LIGO noise with synthetic injections. **[semi]** — [Yan et al., arXiv:2104.03961 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03961)
- Unmodelled transients: GWAK trains five recurrent autoencoders (noise, glitches, BBH, low- and high-frequency sine-Gaussians) and combines their reconstruction losses plus a correlation statistic in a linear classifier; the authors acknowledge an accuracy cost relative to supervised methods in exchange for generality. Its O3 search recovered three known CBCs and a range of glitches; no new astrophysical event is claimed in the abstract. **[real, abstract-only]** — [GWAK, arXiv:2309.11537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11537); [GWAK O3 search, arXiv:2412.19883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19883)
- Foundational caution: Gebhard et al. (2019) argue CNNs alone cannot establish a statistically significant GW detection, are best used as fast trigger generators for follow-up, and can be fooled by inputs with no resemblance to real signals. — [arXiv:1904.08693 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08693)

**Seismology**
- Template matching itself (classical, not ML) set the benchmark for "hidden" events: the QTM catalogue for Southern California (2008-2017) contains 1.81 million earthquakes, about 10x the ~180,000 in the routine catalogue, lowering completeness from ~M1.7 to ~M0.3. **[real]** — [Ross et al. 2019, Science (OSTI record)](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1511259-searching-hidden-earthquakes-southern-california); [Caltech Seismolab](https://seismolab.caltech.edu/news/scientists-identify-almost-2-million-previously-hidden-earthquakes)
- EQTransformer: 56 layers, ~372k trainable parameters, input 60 s x 3 components at 100 Hz, trained on STEAD (~1M earthquake + 300k noise waveforms). On the STEAD test set: detection F1 1.00 (STA/LTA 0.95), P-pick F1 0.99 (PhaseNet 0.96), S-pick F1 0.98 (PhaseNet 0.94). Applied to 5 weeks of the 2000 Tottori sequence (a region absent from training) it located 21,092 events, versus 8,521 in the earlier manual study, using 18 of 57 stations; completeness magnitude 1.50 versus 1.82 for JMA. **[real]** — [Mousavi et al. 2020, Nat. Commun.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7415159)
- PhaseNet-based catalogue for the 2016-2017 Central Italy sequence: 900,058 events in one year from 139 stations, more than a 10-fold increase over the routine catalogue. **[real]** — [Tan et al. 2021, The Seismic Record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10335710); [catalogue on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/4736089)
- Workflow dependence: in the 2019 Durres aftershock study, PhaseNet + HEX association found 3,551 events and EQTransformer 1,110, with completeness ~M1 for the PhaseNet catalogue. Template matching is described as detecting more picks than traditional approaches but being biased toward already-known clusters. **[real, snippet-only]** — [arXiv:2205.12033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12033)
- Ridgecrest 2019 continuous-data benchmark: EQNet2 (multi-station transformer, Oct 2026) 29,058 events; PhaseNO + GaMMA 27,532; PhaseNet+ 24,318; routine SCSN 11,499. **[real, snippet-only; numbers not in the abstract]** — [EQNet2, arXiv:2610.17404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17404)
- The generalised phase detection CNN was trained on 18.2 million hand-picked Southern California seismograms with the stated aim of matching template-matching sensitivity without its bias toward known sources. — [Ross et al., arXiv:1805.01075 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01075)
- Hybrid suggestion for low-frequency earthquakes: use deep learning to find a diverse template set, then template matching to complete each family. — [arXiv:2311.13971 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13971)
- Benchmark across 6 deep pickers and 8 datasets: EQTransformer, GPD and PhaseNet perform best; cross-region transfer costs only mild performance, but transfer between regional and teleseismic data does not work in either direction. — [Munchmeyer et al., arXiv:2110.13671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13671)

**Radio astronomy and SETI**
- Ma et al. 2023 (Nature Astronomy): beta-convolutional VAE + random forest on 820 Hipparcos stars, >480 h of Green Bank data. Trained on 120,000 setigen-injected samples (S/N 10-50, drift within +/-8 Hz/s) on 14,711 real background snippets. Test metrics: precision 0.994, recall 0.985, AUC 0.9993; false-positive rate < 1% at a 90% threshold. Of ~115 million snippet windows, 2,917,789 were flagged; 20,515 survived RFI-band and threshold cuts; visual inspection left 8 signals of interest from 5 stars. None had been identified by the earlier turboSETI searches (which reported 29 and 37 million hits); on average 61-64% of ML events were not in those searches. Re-observation did not re-detect any. **[real + semi]** — [arXiv:2301.12670 full text via ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.12670); [Nature Astronomy](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41550-022-01872-z)
- Ma et al. sensitivity falls with drift rate because of unbalanced training: ~80% at 6 Hz/s, 60% at 8 Hz/s, 25% at 10 Hz/s. — [ar5iv:2301.12670](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.12670)
- FETCH (FRB candidate classifier): 11 CNN models each with accuracy and recall > 99.5% on a test set of real RFI and pulsar single pulses; trained on simulated FRBs and real RFI from Green Bank; recovers all FRBs with S/N > 10 in ASKAP and Parkes data. This is a classifier applied after a classical single-pulse search, so it cannot recover events below the upstream threshold. **[real + semi]** — [Agarwal et al., arXiv:1902.06343](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06343)
- DRAFTS (deep-learning FRB search operating on time-frequency data, replacing dedispersion-then-threshold): in a re-search of FAST data on FRB 20190520B it detected more than three times the bursts found by Heimdall. **[real, snippet-only]** — [Zhang et al., arXiv:2410.03200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03200); the project README instead says it "almost doubled" the count — [DRAFTS GitHub](https://github.com/SukiYume/DRAFTS)
- Pulsar candidates: an ML classifier applied to 17.6 million candidates from GPU reprocessing of archival HTRU data yielded 23 new pulsars (6 millisecond pulsars) — [Morello et al., arXiv:1811.04929 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04929); the SPINN neural selector recovered every known pulsar in cross-validation at 0.64% false-positive rate and found 4 new pulsars — [arXiv:1406.3627 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.3627); a 2023 reprocessing of the Parkes Multibeam survey found 37 more, and its history section notes each successive candidate-classification method reached lower S/N — [arXiv:2302.00255 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00255)

**RF / communications**
- For a fully known signal in white Gaussian noise the matched filter is optimal and is an upper bound on any detector; when parameters are unknown no uniformly optimal Neyman-Pearson detector exists. — [arXiv:2512.13542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13542)
- DetectNet (2 conv layers of 60 filters + 2 LSTM layers of 128 cells + dense layers; trained on RadioML-2016.10a-style data, SNR -20 to +20 dB): at P_fa = 5.92% and P_d = 90% the SNR wall moves from about -5.35 dB (energy detector) to about -8.5 dB; for GFSK at 128 samples, -5.57 dB (energy detector) to -11.00 dB. Generalises within a modulation family (QAM16 to QAM64) but degrades sharply across families (BPSK to GFSK). **[sim]** — [Gao et al., arXiv:1909.02730](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1909.02730)
- A 2025 study with unknown carrier offset (+/-1 kHz) and phase, 500-sample sequences, SNR -30 to +5 dB, P_fa = 0.01: an InceptionTime classifier was ~3 dB better than FFT thresholding for a sine tone, only barely beat the energy detector for QPSK, and did not beat the energy detector for OFDM. One model trained on all three signal types lost < 1 dB versus per-signal models. ML performance fell as signal complexity rose. **[sim]** — [arXiv:2512.13542, HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13542v1)
- A CNN + LSTM spectrum sensor outperforms the energy detector especially at low SNR. — [Sensors 2019 (PMC6567377) [snippet-only]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6567377)
- Modulation recognition (classification, not detection): O'Shea et al. report up to 99.8% accuracy at high SNR with a ResNet on 24 modulations, and 87% retained on over-the-air data after synthetic training. — [arXiv:1712.04578 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04578). Average accuracy on RadioML 2016 across all SNRs is typically only ~57% because low-SNR bins dominate the errors. — [arXiv:2601.02613 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02613)

**Particle-physics anomaly triggers**
- Autoencoders on FPGA for Level-1 triggering (Govorkova et al.): a dense VAE (57 inputs, encoder 32-16-3) runs in 80 ns using 1% DSP / 3% LUT of a Xilinx VU9P with 8-bit post-training quantisation; the dense AE takes 130 ns; the conv AE 1480 ns. AUC is 84-96% for four benchmark signals, but TPR at FPR = 1e-5 is only 0.05-0.13% for three of them and 2.6-6.0% for A -> 4l. **[sim]** — [arXiv:2108.03986](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03986)
- Deployed systems: CMS AXOL1TL (VAE encoder in the Global Trigger since 2024, inputs are pT/eta/phi of 10 jets, 4 e/gamma, 4 muons, MET; latent size 8; score = sum of squared latent means; trained on 2023 ZeroBias data with QKeras quantisation-aware training, converted with hls4ml; Virtex-7) and CICADA (convolutional AE on 18x14 calorimeter images, distilled into a smaller student). AXOL1TL gives a 46% efficiency gain over the rest of the L1 menu for exotic H -> 4b at ~1 kHz, and its rate was reported stable during June 2024 running. **[real]** — [arXiv:2411.19506](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19506v1)
- Other families at the same latency: continuous normalising flow on FPGA at 315 ns using the squared norm of the vector field as score — [arXiv:2508.11594 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11594); distilling flow likelihoods into small BDT/DNN students for O(100 ns) inference — [arXiv:2608.19912 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19912), [arXiv:2609.15295 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15295); decision-tree anomaly detection at nanosecond scale — [arXiv:2304.03836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03836); ATLAS NomAD with sub-25 ns inference — [APS April 2026 abstract [snippet-only]](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/smt/2026/apr-h89/5/)

### Inferences
- The gap to the matched filter is small where the matched filter is truly optimal (single known waveform family, stationary Gaussian noise) and ML's wins come from the parts of the problem the matched filter does not model: glitches, incomplete template banks, look-elsewhere cost of very large banks, and unknown nuisance parameters. A small adaptive model should therefore be tested where the classical baseline is knowingly sub-optimal (non-stationary noise, parameter uncertainty), not against an ideal matched filter in white noise, where the best achievable outcome is parity.
- The real-noise numbers that beat PyCBC (AresGW, Sage) come from 50+ layer ResNets trained on weeks to months of noise. MLGWSC-1 showed small plain CNNs collapsing to zero sensitivity on real noise at 1/month. For a small FPGA model, the glitch/non-Gaussian background, not the faint-signal efficiency, is likely to be the binding constraint.
- In MLGWSC-1 the wavelet excess-power + XGBoost pipeline (cWB) beat every ML submission on real noise (1229 vs 1092 Mpc). A cheap classical front end plus a small learned ranker is a credible low-parameter design, mirroring the 2025 hybrid matched-filter + network result.
- Most "missed events" in seismology are missed by routine catalogues (STA/LTA plus analyst), not by template matching. The QTM template-matching catalogue produced a 10x gain on its own. The evidence that deep pickers beat template matching on events near known sources is thin; their clear advantage is generalising to sources without templates.
- "Classifier after a classical trigger" systems (FETCH, pulsar candidate selectors, the hybrid GW ranker) can only re-rank what the upstream stage emits, so their sensitivity gain comes from lowering the upstream threshold while holding false alarms constant. End-to-end systems (DRAFTS, Aframe, EQTransformer) are the ones that can in principle find sub-threshold events.

### Gaps
- No MLGWSC-2 results were found; one secondary page mentions a roadmap only.
- I could not verify per-SNR accuracy curves for RadioML baselines (e.g. the commonly quoted "near chance below -10 dB") from a primary source.
- The Aframe O4 online paper's quantitative numbers (BNS sensitivity, measured O4 false-alarm behaviour) were not readable beyond the abstract.
- A claim that an EQTransformer-based catalogue exceeds the IPOC catalogue in northern Chile by a factor of 10-15 appeared in a search summary without a pinned source; not verified.
- I found no head-to-head, same-data comparison of a deep picker against a template-matching catalogue with matched false-detection rates.
- Parameter counts are not reported for AresGW, Aframe, Sage or DetectNet in the text I could read.

## 2. Learning strategies for a detector that adapts while deployed

### Takeaway
The strategies with the strongest evidence for faint-signal detectors are label-generating ones that do not depend on the model's own output: continuous injection of synthetic signals into live noise, and delayed labels from a slower, more sensitive pipeline. Replay is the most reliable forgetting control in online settings; unsupervised test-time adaptation and naive self-training are the least safe, with published long-horizon benchmarks showing nearly all such methods eventually underperform a frozen model.

### Cited Findings

**Injection-based self-supervision (synthetic signals into live noise)**
- Aframe generates all training data on the fly: real O3 noise windows sampled independently per detector (so every batch is effectively a new time-slide), random sign inversion and time reversal of noise (p = 0.5 each), simulated waveforms injected with p = 0.277, plus two hard-negative constructions: "muting" (signal in one detector only, labelled noise, p = 0.055) and "swapping" (mismatched waveforms across detectors, labelled noise, p = 0.014). Only ~4.7 days of coincident background were used for training. — [Marx et al., arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18661v2)
- Sage also generates signals on the fly, draws noise at random start times per detector (~1.15e6 independent samples per detector from ~160 days) and recolours noise with other PSDs with 40% probability; its ablation shows that reducing signal and noise variety "significantly degraded detection and caused overfitting". — [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13846v2)
- Ma et al. trained entirely on setigen injections into real backgrounds because no true positives exist. — [ar5iv:2301.12670](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.12670)
- CHIME/FRB runs an injection system in the live pipeline: ~85,000 synthetic FRBs injected into the real-time search to measure selection effects, with injected fluence-to-S/N behaviour matching real detections; the injection datasets are public. This is used for completeness measurement, not training. — [Merryfield et al., arXiv:2206.14079 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14079)

**Curriculum over injected SNR**
- Controlled test (15 strategies x 50 seeds, single detector, Gaussian noise): no significant difference in final performance between decreasing-SNR curricula and fixed-range training; networks trained on low-SNR signals generalise to high SNR but not the reverse; fastest convergence when low-SNR samples appear early; recommendation is a fixed low-SNR range. — [Schafer et al., arXiv:2106.03741](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2106.03741)
- Earlier studies disagree: George & Huerta reported a noticeable gain from curriculum, Gabbard et al. none. — [Schafer et al., arXiv:2106.03741 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03741)
- In practice production-oriented systems still use one: Aframe lowers the minimum SNR from 12 to 4 over 989 batches (power-law SNR, max 100); AresGW starts at SNR >= 7.63 for four epochs and uses all signals after epoch ten. — [arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18661v2); [arXiv:2211.01520](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.01520)
- Trade-off: training on low-SNR events degrades noise rejection and raises false-alarm rate, so one early-warning study stopped lowering SNR and sampled uniformly in later stages. — [arXiv:2104.09438 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09438). Sage sets a lower bound of optimal SNR 5 to avoid signal/noise class overlap acting as label noise. — [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13846v2)
- Denoising autoencoders do benefit: starting at high SNR and lowering gradually gave lower error than training directly at low SNR. — [arXiv:1903.03105 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03105)

**Delayed-label and teacher-student setups**
- Delayed labels are studied as "verification latency": performance can only be updated when labels arrive, and under drift a label can be stale on arrival. Latency is classed as null, intermediate, or extreme (labels never arrive). — [active learning for streams survey, arXiv:2302.08893 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893); [extreme verification latency comparison, arXiv:2011.14917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.14917)
- A survey of semi-supervised learning for delayed, partially labelled streams notes that the combination of delay and partial labelling is under-explored. — [arXiv:2106.09170 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09170)
- Semi-supervised teacher-student in seismology: PhaseNet-DAS v1 is trained on pseudo-labels from a seismometer-trained PhaseNet, and v2 on pseudo-labels from v1, in the Noisy Student style, to move a picker to a new sensor type with no manual labels. **[real]** — [Zhu et al. 2023, Nat. Commun. [snippet-only]](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-43355-3)
- Slow-sensitive-stage plus fast-ranker examples: the 2025 GW hybrid (matched-filter bank feeds a network) — [arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516); CICADA's knowledge distillation of a larger autoencoder into a trigger-sized student — [arXiv:2411.19506](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19506v1); distillation of normalising-flow likelihoods into small FPGA students — [arXiv:2608.19912 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19912)
- Continual top-up versus periodic retrain has been studied for a CMS trigger model (track/vertex finding): episodic continual updates are compared against full retrain-and-redeploy as conditions change, with the note that the approach needs a continual stream of labelled truth. Conclusions not readable. — [EPJ Web Conf. 295, 09037 [snippet-only]](https://www.epj-conferences.org/articles/epjconf/pdf/2024/05/epjconf_chep2024_09037.pdf)

**Continual learning and forgetting control**
- EWC slows learning on weights important to earlier tasks. — [Kirkpatrick et al., arXiv:1612.00796](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796). In the online single-pass setting, EWC performs almost identically to plain fine-tuning and is described as ill-suited. — [search summary of online-CL literature, see arXiv:2501.04897 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04897)
- Empirical online-continual-learning survey: replay-based MIR is the most versatile method across class- and domain-incremental settings; GDumb does poorly in domain-incremental settings; iCaRL is competitive with small buffers. — [Mai et al., arXiv:2101.10423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10423)
- Very small replay buffers help: gains of 7-17% with a single stored example per class, and repeated training on tiny memories does not harm generalisation. — [Chaudhry et al., arXiv:1902.10486 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486). Sample selection matters more as the buffer shrinks. — [arXiv:2108.06758 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06758)
- Parameter-isolation in streams: MAGIC Net (2026) learns real-valued masks over frozen weights when an external drift detector fires. — [arXiv:2603.08600 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08600)
- "Streaming continual learning" as the union of stream learning and continual learning is surveyed in — [IJCAI 2023 survey](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/743)

**Test-time adaptation (TTA) to non-stationary noise**
- On a long continually-changing-corruption benchmark, all but one state-of-the-art TTA method eventually collapse and do worse than the unadapted model; simply resetting to the pretrained weights periodically (RDumb) matches or beats prior methods. — [Press et al., arXiv:2306.05401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05401)
- Entropy-minimisation TTA (Tent, ETA) can fail at batch size 1; error rates above 97% are reported for very small batches. — [EATA, arXiv:2204.02610 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02610); [REALM, arXiv:2309.03964 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03964). SAR stabilises this by filtering samples with large noisy gradients and using sharpness-aware updates, and works with group/layer norm at batch size 1. — [SAR, arXiv:2302.12400 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12400)
- Mitigations in the literature: stochastic restoration of source weights (CoTTA), Fisher regularisation plus sample rejection (EATA). — [arXiv:2311.04991 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04991); surveys: [online TTA survey, arXiv:2310.20199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20199), [continual TTA survey (TMLR 2026), arXiv:2607.08164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08164)
- Learned input normalisation as lightweight adaptation: AresGW's Deep Adaptive Input Normalization adjusts shift/scale/gating per input at inference and outperformed batch norm at low FAR. — [arXiv:2211.01520](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.01520)
- How much drift matters in practice for GW: an Aframe model trained on the first 10 days of O3 kept a stable detected fraction of SNR > 8 injections at FAR 1 per 2 months across the following ~10 months, with week-to-week fluctuation and no clear decay; the authors say a model "can continue to be used for months without retraining" but note this metric does not capture glitch-rate or morphology changes. — [arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18661v2). PSD mismatch in whitening can cost up to 20% sensitivity in a worst realistic case. — [arXiv:2106.03741](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2106.03741)
- Supervised domain adaptation is data-cheap in seismology: retraining with 3,500 seismograms (0.45% of the original training set) adapted a picker to hydraulic-fracturing data. — [Chai et al. 2020 [snippet-only]](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/using-a-deep-neural-network-and-transfer-learning-to-bridge-scale/). PhaseNet/EQTransformer drop in performance on Chinese data and were fixed by regional fine-tuning. — [Zhu et al. 2023, Earthquake Science [snippet-only]](https://doaj.org/article/62ec5a4d795244b0ba4189b539a3ee05)

**Concept-drift detection**
- The standard framing separates drift detection, drift understanding (when/how/where) and drift adaptation; adaptive windowing (ADWIN) picks window size from the data. — [Lu et al., "Learning under Concept Drift: A Review", arXiv:2004.05785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05785)

**Positive-unlabelled (PU) learning**
- Most PU methods assume labelled positives are Selected Completely At Random (SCAR), so Pr(labelled | x) = c * Pr(positive | x) and a classifier trained on labelled-vs-unlabelled can be rescaled by the label frequency c; wrongly assuming SCAR hurts performance, motivating the weaker Selected-At-Random assumption. — [Bekker & Davis survey, arXiv:1811.04820](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04820)

**Anomaly / novelty detection**
- Autoencoder and VAE scores (reconstruction error; KL term or sum of squared latent means so only the encoder runs) are the deployed option at nanosecond latency. — [arXiv:2108.03986](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03986); [arXiv:2411.19506](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19506v1)
- Semi-supervised embedding of several autoencoders (GWAK) trades supervised accuracy for coverage of unmodelled signals. — [arXiv:2309.11537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11537)
- Normalising flows at 315 ns on FPGA. — [arXiv:2508.11594 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11594)

**Hard-example mining**
- OHEM replaces uniform sampling with sampling by current per-example loss, removing hand-tuned heuristics for hard negatives. — [Shrivastava et al., arXiv:1604.03540](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03540)
- Domain-specific hard negatives are constructed rather than mined: Aframe's muting/swapping; ~30% of training samples containing glitches in the GW hybrid search; Sage finds that keeping glitches in training noise helps reject unseen glitches. — [arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18661v2); [arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516); [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13846v2)

**Active learning with a human or pipeline in the loop**
- Stream-based active learning with slow oracles: when the expert's answer is delayed, queries pile up; PRopagate estimates utility independent of latency by also predicting pending labels. — [arXiv:2204.06822 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06822); [survey, arXiv:2302.08893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893)
- Human-guided discovery of new glitch classes via similarity learning on a transfer-learned feature space (Gravity Spy). — [arXiv:1903.04058 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04058)

**On-device learning hardware context (brief)**
- hls4ml is primarily an inference compiler — [arXiv:2512.01463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01463); a 2025 extension (ENABOL) adds on-chip backpropagation and weight updates and names continual learning and adaptive edge detectors as targets — [FastML 2025 slides [snippet-only]](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1496673/contributions/6637920/attachments/3127033/5547241/2025%20-%20FastML%20-%20ENABOL.pdf); a 2026 preprint claims the first model-free online learning at sub-microsecond latency on FPGA using Kolmogorov-Arnold networks, arguing their sparse local spline updates and quantisation robustness beat MLPs under fixed-point training — [arXiv:2602.02056 [abstract-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02056)

### Inferences
- Injection-based training is the natural label source for an adaptive faint-signal detector: labels are exact, amplitude is controlled, and the noise is whatever the instrument is currently producing. It trains sensitivity to the simulated morphology only, so it should be paired with a second label source (delayed teacher labels) if real signals differ from the simulator.
- A delayed-label teacher gives labels that are biased toward the events the teacher can see. In PU terms the labelled positives are not selected completely at random (loud events are over-represented), so SCAR-based corrections will be wrong; the label propensity depends on SNR and should be modelled or measured with injections through the teacher.
- The curriculum evidence suggests that for an online system the useful knob is the lower SNR bound of injections, not the schedule: lowering it buys faint-signal efficiency until class overlap starts raising false alarms. An experiment can treat "minimum injected SNR" as the controlled variable and read out efficiency at fixed FAR.
- Given RDumb and the EWC result, the low-risk adaptive design is: supervised updates from injections on live noise, a small replay buffer (including stored hard negatives and old-noise samples), a frozen reference copy of the weights, and periodic or drift-triggered reset/rollback. Unsupervised entropy-style TTA is a poor fit, especially at batch size 1 on a stream.
- The Aframe stability result means that on GW-like data, noise drift alone may give little headroom for adaptation to show a benefit over months; the experiment needs either a noise source with real regime changes (glitch classes, RFI environment, PSD changes) or a deliberately induced shift.
- Normalisation-only adaptation (updating whitening/PSD estimates or a learned input normalisation layer) is a cheap first rung that should be the baseline against which weight adaptation is compared.

### Gaps
- I found no published system that closes the full loop on real data: a fast deployed detector that is updated online from a slower pipeline's later labels of events it missed, with before/after sensitivity measured at fixed FAR. The closest pieces are offline (PhaseNet-DAS pseudo-labelling, hybrid GW ranker, CMS continual top-up).
- No 2025-2026 paper applying streaming continual learning directly to radio or seismic event detection was found.
- No source was found that applies positive-unlabelled learning to GW, seismic or radio event detection; the PU material is from the general ML literature.
- Online hard-example mining and focal loss were not found applied to glitch or false-positive suppression in these domains; hard negatives are hand-constructed instead.
- Contrastive anomaly detection at trigger latency: nothing found.
- Results of the CMS continual-learning-versus-retrain comparison and ENABOL performance numbers could not be read.
- Whether Aframe was retrained during O4, and how its online background was maintained, was not found.

## 3. Failure modes and pitfalls, and how published work guards against them

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are false alarms from non-Gaussian noise rather than missed faint signals, optimistic metrics from train/test leakage or single-month test sets, numerical saturation of the ranking statistic at very low false-alarm rates, and collapse when a model adapts on its own outputs. Published guards are disjoint-time splits, large time-slide backgrounds, constructed hard negatives, glitch vetoes or consistency checks, frozen-model fallbacks, and follow-up confirmation.

### Cited Findings
- **Self-training collapse / confirmation bias.** Naive pseudo-labelling overfits to its own wrong labels; mitigations are mixup, a minimum number of true-labelled samples per mini-batch, and an EMA teacher separate from the student. — [Arazo et al., arXiv:1908.02983](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02983). Long-run TTA collapses below the frozen baseline, including methods designed to prevent it. — [RDumb, arXiv:2306.05401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05401). Continual-TTA surveys name error accumulation from noisy pseudo-labels and forgetting of source knowledge as the two core failure modes. — [arXiv:2607.08164 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08164)
- **False-alarm inflation on real noise.** Two MLGWSC-1 CNNs that reached 40-95% of PyCBC on Gaussian noise had zero sensitive distance on real noise at 1/month because glitches dominated their triggers. — [MLGWSC-1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146). In the SETI search ~2.9 million snippets were flagged, the authors state most are likely RFI, and none of the final signals was re-detected. — [ar5iv:2301.12670](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.12670)
- **Low-SNR training raises false alarms.** Reported explicitly as a trade-off in — [arXiv:2104.09438 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09438); Sage treats low-SNR class overlap as label noise and floors injected SNR at 5 — [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13846v2)
- **Ranking-statistic saturation.** A final softmax saturates to exactly 1 in floating point, which made efficiency collapse to zero below ~1e3 false alarms/month; removing it and thresholding the difference of the linear outputs ("unbounded softmax replacement") fixed it. — [Schafer et al., arXiv:2106.03741](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2106.03741). AresGW runs its output layer in float64 at inference and its log-statistic is still capped at 16 by double precision. — [arXiv:2407.07820](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07820v1)
- **Overfitting to simulated morphology and to the training prior.** Sage catalogues 11 biases, including: under-representation of long/low-chirp-mass signals when sampling uniformly in component mass (fix: sample in chirp-time coordinates); spectral bias toward low frequencies (fix: whitening); too little noise/signal variety; train-test mismatch in PSDs; and train-test overlap of noise realisations. AresGW and Aframe are said to be affected by the mass-prior bias, which narrows their effective parameter space. — [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13846v2). AresGW's own recovery numbers show it: 34/43 known events inside the training chirp-mass range, 10/55 outside. — [arXiv:2407.07820](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07820v1). Ma et al.'s sensitivity drops to 25% at the under-trained drift rate of 10 Hz/s. — [ar5iv:2301.12670](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.12670). DetectNet fails to transfer across modulation families. — [arXiv:1909.02730](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1909.02730)
- **Metric artefacts.** The volume-weighted sensitive-distance metric weights each found injection by (chirp mass)^(5/2), so a model biased toward heavy systems can score a higher sensitive distance while detecting fewer signals than matched filtering at every FAR; a Sage variant found 15.5% more signals than AresGW but scored 3.5% lower. — [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13846v2). Sensitivity measured on one month of real noise shows "notable performance variations" between months, attributed to dataset variability and finite duration. — [arXiv:2509.05283 [abstract-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05283). MFCNN's apparent sensitivity in MLGWSC-1 fell sharply when background was estimated from the foreground data instead of the separate background set. — [MLGWSC-1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146)
- **AUC hides low-FPR behaviour.** L1 autoencoders with AUC 84-96% have TPR of only ~0.1% at FPR 1e-5 for three of four signals. — [arXiv:2108.03986](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03986)
- **Adversarial / non-physical triggers.** Inputs with no resemblance to GW signals can be classified as signals. — [Gebhard et al., arXiv:1904.08693 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08693)
- **Train/test leakage through noise.** Guards in use: disjoint calendar periods for train/validation/test (Sage; Aframe excludes its 17-day training period from the O3 sensitivity estimate); per-detector independent noise sampling and segment swapping (AresGW replaces the L1 segment with another with p = 0.4). Sage acknowledges its best run recoloured training noise with PSDs estimated from the test segment, a mild overlap. — [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13846v2); [arXiv:2505.21261 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21261); [arXiv:2211.01520](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.01520)
- **Background drift.** The GW hybrid search re-estimates its background at intervals through O3a/O3b to handle drift in the ranking-statistic distribution, and uses different tuned thresholds for O3a and O3b. — [arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516)
- **Limited background and extrapolation.** With ~1.5 years of time-slide background, FARs below ~1 per 2 months come from a linear fit to the background tail (hybrid search). Aframe's first paper has a FAR floor of 1/yr because only one year of background was analysed. AresGW uses analytic FAR fits per trigger class over a 10-year time-shifted background. — [arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516); [arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18661v2); [arXiv:2407.07820](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07820v1)
- **Independent confirmation is weak for ML-only candidates.** AresGW's 8 unique O3 candidates were not found by Aframe (0 unique). — [arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516)
- **PU assumption violations.** Assuming labelled positives are a random sample of positives when they are not degrades the classifier. — [Bekker & Davis, arXiv:1811.04820](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04820)
- **Regularisation that does not work online.** EWC behaves like plain fine-tuning in the single-pass online setting. — [arXiv:2501.04897 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04897)

### Inferences
- For an adaptive detector the false-alarm rate is not a fixed property of a threshold: every weight update changes the null distribution of the ranking statistic, so the threshold must be recalibrated against fresh background after each update, or the "improved sensitivity" may simply be a lowered effective threshold. The hybrid GW search's periodic background refresh is the published analogue.
- Trials/look-elsewhere: evaluating many model snapshots and reporting the best one is a selection effect. The clean protocol fixes the update rule in advance and evaluates the whole trajectory on held-out data; I found no paper that states a trials-factor correction for adaptively updated detectors (see Gaps).
- AresGW's three trigger classes with separate FAR fits, and any scheme that picks the best of several filters per trigger, multiplies the effective number of trials; a small-model experiment should use a single ranking statistic.
- Fixed-point arithmetic on an FPGA makes the saturation problem worse than in float32: the ranking statistic's dynamic range in the far noise tail needs explicit bit-width budgeting (use pre-activation logits, not a sigmoid/softmax output).
- If the adaptive model learns from its own above-threshold detections, it will reinforce loud, already-detectable morphologies and will not move the threshold for faint ones. Injection labels and teacher labels avoid this by construction; own-detection labels should be excluded or heavily down-weighted.

### Gaps
- No explicit treatment of look-elsewhere/trials factors for ML detectors with multiple model versions or adaptive updates was found.
- The quantitative month-to-month variation in sensitive distance from arXiv:2509.05283 could not be extracted (abstract only); the only number found is AresGW's own +/-3% at 1/month.
- No study was found measuring how a detector's false-alarm rate evolves under online weight updates on real instrument noise.

## 4. Evaluation practice for faint-signal detectors and honest before/after comparison for an adaptive model

### Takeaway
The accepted standard is injection-recovery at a fixed false-alarm rate with background measured empirically on signal-free or time-shifted real data: report detection efficiency versus SNR (or sensitive distance/volume versus FAR), not accuracy or AUC. For an adaptive model the comparison must hold FAR fixed by recalibrating after every update, use test noise disjoint in time from anything the model trained on, and include frozen, periodically retrained, and reset baselines.

### Cited Findings
- **FAR and sensitive distance (MLGWSC-1 protocol).** FAR = number of background triggers above a ranking-statistic threshold divided by background duration. A separate foreground stream has injections; an injection is "found" if a trigger above threshold lies within +/-Delta-t of it. Sensitive volume is a Monte-Carlo estimate over found injections with a chirp-mass weighting to correct for the injection distance distribution, converted to a sphere radius. Reported as sensitive distance versus FAR, down to one per month from one month of background. Wall-clock runtime is reported alongside. — [MLGWSC-1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146)
- **Time-slides for background.** Shifting one detector's data by more than the inter-site light travel time (~10 ms) destroys real coincidences and multiplies effective background time: 100 shifts of one week gives ~1.5 years (hybrid search); Aframe shifts in 1 s increments to build one year, then 100 years, of background; AresGW builds 10 years from 18 realisations of 6.7 months. Known event times are removed from the background. — [arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516); [arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18661v2); [arXiv:2505.21261 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21261); [arXiv:2407.07820](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07820v1)
- **Sensitive volume definition (Aframe).** The volume over which a population distributed uniformly in comoving volume is detectable at a given FAR, estimated by injecting simulated signals into strain and analysing time-slide background. Aframe generated ~45 million test waveforms, rejection-sampled to SNR > 4. — [arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18661v2)
- **Stability-over-time metric.** Fraction of SNR > 8 injections detected at FAR 1 per 2 months, tracked week by week for ten months after training. — [arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18661v2)
- **Count detections as well as volume.** Sage reports the number of found injections at each FAR alongside sensitive distance because the volume metric over-weights high-mass systems; it reports the mean of three seeds with min/max bars. — [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13846v2)
- **Variance across test sets.** +/-3% in sensitive distance at 1/month across test sets (AresGW); a dedicated 2025 study recommends more rigorous statistical validation across multiple month-long datasets. — [arXiv:2211.01520](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.01520); [arXiv:2509.05283 [abstract-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05283)
- **Astrophysical probability.** p_astro from foreground and background models of the ranking statistic; detection thresholds such as p_astro >= 0.5 and FAR < 2/day. — [arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516); [arXiv:2407.07820](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07820v1)
- **RF convention.** Probability of detection versus SNR at fixed probability of false alarm (e.g. P_fa = 0.01 +/- 0.001 with 1,000 sequences per 1 dB bin from -30 to +5 dB), bracketed by the energy detector and the matched filter; "SNR wall" = SNR at which P_d reaches 90% at the stated P_f. — [arXiv:2512.13542](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13542v1); [arXiv:1909.02730](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1909.02730)
- **Trigger convention.** TPR at a fixed very low FPR (1e-5, tied to the affordable trigger rate) for each benchmark signal, plus rate stability over running time. — [arXiv:2108.03986](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03986); [arXiv:2411.19506](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19506v1)
- **Seismology convention.** Event counts against a reference catalogue, magnitude of completeness (1.50 vs 1.82 at Tottori; M0.3 vs M1.7 for QTM), pick residuals against manual picks (std ~0.08 s), and precision/recall/F1 on labelled windows. — [EQTransformer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7415159); [Ross et al. 2019](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1511259-searching-hidden-earthquakes-southern-california)
- **Survey completeness by live injection.** CHIME/FRB forward-models synthetic bursts through the real-time pipeline to measure completeness as a function of intrinsic burst properties; reused for Catalog 2's selection function. — [arXiv:2206.14079 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14079); [arXiv:2606.26334 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26334)
- **Delayed-label evaluation for streams.** Test-then-train assumes immediate labels; "continuous re-evaluation" instead scores the sequence of predictions made for an instance between its arrival and the arrival of its label. — [Grzenda, Gomes & Bifet 2020](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/delayed-labelling-evaluation-for-data-streams/); [IJCNN 2020 preprint](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/bitstream/10289/14000/8/IJCNN_Delayed_Labeling_2020-preprint.pdf)
- **Adaptation baselines.** A non-adapting model and a periodic-reset model are the baselines that long-horizon TTA methods fail to beat. — [arXiv:2306.05401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05401). TTA should also be evaluated under compute-time constraints, since slower methods see fewer samples on a real stream. — [arXiv:2304.04795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04795)

### Inferences
An honest before/after protocol for an adaptive faint-signal detector, assembled from the practices above:
- Split the noise stream by time into adaptation periods and evaluation periods that the model never trains on; evaluation injections use a fixed, pre-registered set (same waveforms, same times, same amplitudes) replayed for every model version.
- After each update, re-measure the background ranking-statistic distribution on signal-free held-out noise (time-slides if multi-channel) and choose the threshold that gives the target FAR. Compare models at equal FAR, never at equal threshold.
- Report efficiency versus injected SNR (the curve, plus SNR at 50% and 90% efficiency) and number of found injections at several FARs, with uncertainties from multiple seeds and multiple evaluation periods. The background duration sets the lowest FAR that can be claimed: with T seconds of background, claims below roughly 1/T need extrapolation and should be labelled as such.
- Baselines: (a) frozen initial model; (b) frozen model with only normalisation/whitening updated; (c) periodic full retrain from scratch on all data to date (upper reference); (d) periodic reset to initial weights; (e) classical bound: matched filter with known parameters and energy/excess-power detector on the same injections.
- Report forward performance (on the newest noise) and backward performance (on the earliest noise) to expose forgetting, and track the false-alarm rate at the fixed deployed threshold between recalibrations to expose drift-induced inflation.
- For delayed-label variants, account for the labelling delay in the timeline: a label that arrives after a regime change should be scored as used when it arrived, not when the event occurred.
- Because the Aframe result shows months of stability without retraining on GW data, an adaptation benefit needs to exceed the +/-3% test-set variance seen at 1/month to be credible; plan the number of evaluation periods accordingly.

### Gaps
- No published protocol specifically for before/after evaluation of an online-adapting event detector at fixed FAR was found; the list above is a synthesis.
- I did not find a source quantifying how many injections or how much background are needed to resolve a given percentage change in efficiency at a given FAR.

## 5. Public datasets and simulators for a streaming experiment

### Takeaway
For a small, very-low-latency model with controlled-SNR injections, the most practical first choices are (a) self-generated RF/IQ streams (TorchSig or a simple AWGN generator, with RadioML as a fixed benchmark), where SNR is exact and matched-filter and energy-detector bounds are analytic, and (b) the MLGWSC-1 generator on GWOSC O3a strain, which gives real non-stationary noise, controlled injections and published classical and ML baselines. The ADC2021 set is the natural fit for nanosecond hls4ml models but is not a time series and has no SNR knob.

### Inferences
- Best fits for a small model with tight latency and exact SNR control:
  1. **Self-generated IQ streams (TorchSig or a custom AWGN + impairment generator).** Short frames (128-1024 samples), exact SNR, analytic matched-filter and energy-detector reference curves, existing FPGA modulation-classification precedents at microsecond latency, and non-stationarity can be injected deliberately (noise-floor steps, interferers). RadioML 2018.01A works as a frozen external benchmark but its non-commercial licence and lack of a noise-only class limit it.
  2. **MLGWSC-1 generator on GWOSC O3a.** Real glitchy, drifting noise at 2048 Hz, injection amplitude set by distance, and published baselines at fixed FAR. Costs: tens of GB of strain, 1 s+ context windows, and the documented collapse of small CNNs on dataset 4, which makes it a hard but informative second-stage test.
  3. **STEAD/INSTANCE noise traces + scaled event traces.** 100 Hz, three channels, ~100-130k real noise windows, permissive licence; SNR control is by amplitude scaling of real events (semi-synthetic), and the QTM/routine catalogue pair offers a natural delayed-teacher experiment.
- ADC2021 matches the hls4ml toolchain and the ~100 ns latency class directly, but the "signal" is a different physics process, not a faint version of a known waveform, so it tests anomaly detection, not SNR-threshold lowering.
- Breakthrough Listen + setigen supports controlled S/N and drift-rate injection into real RFI-laden data, but inputs are 2-D spectrograms and files are large; better as a later generalisation test.
- G2Net Kaggle data are pre-windowed with hidden SNR, so they cannot support streaming or efficiency-versus-SNR evaluation.

### Gaps
- G2Net 2021 sample counts (commonly quoted 560,000 train / 226,000 test) could not be confirmed from a source I could read.
- Licences not confirmed: MLGWSC-1 repository licence type, O4a strain, Sig53/WidebandSig53 data, Breakthrough Listen archive, CHIME/FRB products, ADC2021 Zenodo records.
- TorchSig's current API for specifying exact SNR, and the current names/sizes of its packaged narrowband and wideband datasets, were not verified.
- I found no public continuous FRB intensity stream suitable for a streaming detector test; public CHIME products are catalogues, per-burst baseband and injection metadata.
- Whether SeisBench exposes a ready-made controlled-SNR mixing augmentation was not verified.
- Current size of the Breakthrough Listen archive and whether the beta search interface is live were not checked.

## 6. Model families that work at low SNR with small parameter counts

### Takeaway
Demonstrated small models span about 2.5k-5k weights (dense autoencoders at ~100 ns on FPGA), ~29k-372k (compact seismic CNN/attention detectors), and a few stacked conv + LSTM layers for RF detection; the GW detectors that hold up on real noise are much larger 1-D ResNets (34-54+ layers). Evidence favours building domain structure into small models (whitening or adaptive normalisation, matched-filter or multiscale front ends, classical excess-power front ends) over adding depth.

### Cited Findings
- **Dense autoencoders / VAEs (trigger scale).** 57 inputs -> 32 -> 16 -> 3 (mirror decoder); VAE deployed encoder-only with KL-based score; 8-bit weights; 80 ns latency, 1% DSP / 3% LUT on VU9P; conv variants cost 365-1480 ns and up to 47% LUT. Parameter counts not stated in the paper. — [arXiv:2108.03986](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.03986). Deployed AXOL1TL uses latent size 8 and the sum of squared latent means as score. — [arXiv:2411.19506](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19506v1)
- **Compact seismic detectors.** EQTransformer ~372k parameters (56 layers; CNN + BiLSTM + attention), 6,000-sample 3-channel input. — [Mousavi et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7415159). LEQNet cuts EQTransformer's size by 87.68% with comparable performance. — [LEQNet, Frontiers 2022 [snippet-only]](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.848237). LightEQ: 29k parameters, F1 0.99 for detection, 193 kB RAM, runs on microcontrollers. — [Zainab et al., IoTDI 2023 [snippet-only]](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576842.3582387). TinyML earthquake detectors on a microcontroller generalise to events outside the training set. — [Clements, SRL 2023 [snippet-only]](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70263407)
- **1-D CNNs for GW.** Gabbard-style CNN (6 conv + 3 dense, 1 s at 2048 Hz) reaches >= 91.5% of matched-filter distance in Gaussian noise **[sim]**. — [arXiv:2106.03741](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2106.03741). The same class of small CNN had zero sensitivity on real noise at 1/month in MLGWSC-1, where a ~52-54-layer ResNet remained usable. — [MLGWSC-1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146)
- **1-D ResNets.** AresGW: 54 layers, 2 x 2048 input, DAIN front layer. Aframe: ResNet34 with GroupNorm, 1.5 s at 2048 Hz, < 10 ms GPU inference; its kernel length of 3 is blamed for weak low-mass (long-signal) performance. — [arXiv:2211.01520](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.01520); [arXiv:2403.18661](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18661v2)
- **Inductive bias beats size.** In Sage's ablation a plain 1-D ResNet-50 and a deeper ResNet-152 both performed much worse than the multiscale-front-end + attention design at similar or larger parameter count. — [arXiv:2501.13846](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13846v2)
- **Learned matched-filter banks.** MFCNN uses matched-filter layers with 25 templates as learnable weights and a whitening layer ahead of a MobileNetV3 backbone; it was the weakest ML entry at low FAR in MLGWSC-1 (356-779 Mpc) with an unexplained steep sensitivity curve. — [MLGWSC-1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146). MNet-Shallow/Deep implement exact matched filtering at initialisation and improve with training on real noise. — [Yan et al., arXiv:2104.03961 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03961)
- **Classical bank + small ranker.** Matched-filter SNR time series of the top-10 templates per second fed to a network (exported to ONNX, run on CPU). — [arXiv:2512.04516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04516). Wavelet excess-power + XGBoost (cWB) was the best non-templated method on real noise in MLGWSC-1. — [MLGWSC-1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11146)
- **Conv + recurrent for RF detection.** DetectNet: 2 conv layers (60 filters, length 10) + 2 LSTM layers (128 cells) + dense; inputs 64-1024 IQ samples; 3-5 dB better SNR wall than energy detection. — [arXiv:1909.02730](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1909.02730)
- **Recurrent nets on FPGA.** Multi-layer LSTMs for GW time series on Zynq 7045 / Alveo U250 with balanced initiation intervals: up to 42% fewer DSPs and 4.9-12.4x lower latency than prior FPGA LSTM designs. — [Que et al., arXiv:2106.14089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14089). GWAK uses recurrent autoencoders. — [arXiv:2309.11537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11537)
- **Streaming CNNs for modulation classification on FPGA.** RFSoC implementation with ~8 us latency and 488k classifications/s using VGG10/ResNet33-derived quantised nets. — [Tridgell et al. [snippet-only]](https://sidmontu.github.io/pubs_pdfs/amc_raw20.pdf). Quantisation-aware (Brevitas) streaming CNN at 18/16/8/4-bit weights on RFSoC. — [MacLellan et al., IEEE OJCS 2024 [snippet-only]](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/91541/7/MacLellan-etal-IEEE-OJCS-2024-RFSoC-modulation-classification-with-streaming-CNN.pdf)
- **Time-series classifiers for IQ detection.** InceptionTime outperformed MiniRocket for detection with unknown frequency/phase; one model covering three signal types cost < 1 dB. — [arXiv:2512.13542](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13542v1)
- **Non-MLP learners for on-chip training.** Kolmogorov-Arnold networks claimed more efficient and quantisation-robust than MLPs for fixed-point on-FPGA online learning. — [arXiv:2602.02056 [abstract-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02056). Hyperdimensional-computing continual learner on FPGA: up to 40.4x speedup and 383x energy efficiency over CPU/GPU baselines on CORe50. — [ImageHD, arXiv:2604.21280 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21280)
- **Decision trees and distilled students.** Nanosecond anomaly detection with decision trees; BDT/DNN students regressing normalising-flow likelihoods at O(100 ns). — [arXiv:2304.03836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03836); [arXiv:2608.19912 [snippet-only]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19912)

### Inferences
- Parameter count of the trigger-scale dense autoencoder, computed from the stated layer widths (my arithmetic, not reported by the authors): encoder 57->32->16->3 is 1,856 + 528 + 51 = 2,435 weights and biases; the full autoencoder with mirrored decoder is about 4,900. LEQNet's 87.68% reduction from ~372k implies roughly 46k parameters (also my arithmetic).
- A plausible low-latency architecture supported by the evidence: fixed or slowly adapted whitening/normalisation -> small bank of 1-D convolutional kernels initialised as matched filters or multiscale wavelets -> a few thousand to tens of thousands of dense/conv weights producing an unbounded logit. Online adaptation can then be restricted to the normalisation and the final layers, which limits forgetting and keeps update cost small.
- Long-duration signals need receptive field, not depth: Aframe's and TPI's failures on long signals were attributed to short kernels/windows, and the 2026 BNS extension solved it by heterodyning (a preprocessing change) while keeping the same network. For an FPGA model, preprocessing that compresses the signal in time is cheaper than a larger network.
- The step from Gaussian to real noise cost small CNNs everything at low FAR in MLGWSC-1, so a small model's viability depends on pairing it with explicit glitch handling (constructed hard negatives, coincidence between channels, or a classical veto) more than on its architecture family.

### Gaps
- No source was retrieved on wavelet-scattering front ends, temporal convolutional networks (TCN/WaveNet-style), or SincNet-style learnable filterbanks for low-SNR detection; these families are not covered by cited evidence here.
- No published efficiency-versus-SNR curve was found for any model under ~10k parameters on a time-series detection task; the trigger-scale autoencoder results are on event-level physics features.
- Parameter counts for DetectNet, AresGW, Aframe and Sage are not stated in the text I could read; PhaseNet's parameter count was not retrieved.
- Accuracy-versus-SNR tables for quantised FPGA modulation classifiers were not extracted (several report only high-SNR accuracy).
