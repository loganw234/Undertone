# Alveo U50 as a platform for low-latency real-time ML on streaming signal data (hardware, tooling, HBM, signal ingest, capacity)

Research date: 2026-11-21. Verification levels used below: "(fetched)" = I read the page/PDF itself; "(search summary)" = the fact came from a search-result digest of that URL and was not opened in full. Arithmetic I did myself is confined to the Inferences sub-sections. Items marked **[RECENT CHANGE]** changed in 2024-2026.

## 1. Exact hardware: FPGA device, resources, shell overhead, HBM, I/O, power/cooling, clocks

### Takeaway
The U50 carries a two-SLR XCU50 (Virtex UltraScale+ HBM class, part `XCU50-2FSVH2104E`, -2 speed grade) with 872K LUTs, 1,743K registers, 5,952 DSP slices, 47.3 Mb BRAM, 180.0 Mb URAM, 8 GB of in-package HBM2 (no DDR4), one QSFP28 and a 16-lane PCIe edge, all inside a passively cooled 75 W slot-powered half-height card. The standard Vitis XDMA shell leaves roughly 81-85% of each resource type to user logic; the HBM is throttled by a 10 W rail (201 GB/s nominal, 316 GB/s peak) and is attached only to SLR0.

### Cited Findings

**FPGA device**
- The card "features the XCU50 FPGA"; the XCU50 has two super logic regions (SLR0, SLR1) and SLR0 integrates the HBM2 controller for the adjacent 8 GB HBM2 (fetched) — [DS965 FPGA Resource Information](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/FPGA-Resource-Information)
- Part numbers: A-U50 = `XCU50-2FSVH2104E`, Vivado part `XCU50-FSVH2104-2-E`; A-U50-LV = `XCU50-L2SVH2104E`, Vivado part `XCU50-FSVH2104-2LV-E`. Alveo parts are not visible in the Vivado part-selection GUI; a bare-part project must be created with `create_project ... -part` in Tcl, or by selecting the board after installing board files (search summary of UG1371) — [UG1371 Creating a Vivado RTL Project](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1371-u50-reconfig-accel/Creating-a-Vivado-RTL-Project)
- Corundum describes the device as "Xilinx Alveo U50 (Xilinx Virtex UltraScale+ XCU50)" (fetched) — [Corundum README](https://github.com/corundum/corundum)
- A 2022 user blog identifies the U50 silicon as an XCVU35P equivalent and reports that the bare XCVU35P part was rejected by the free Vivado edition while targeting the au50 board worked (search summary; single anecdotal source) — [Vivado ML Standard on an Osprey ECU50](https://josephcarragher.github.io/blogs/ecu50guide.html)

**Resource counts (whole device, DS965 product table, fetched)** — [DS965 Product Details](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details)

- Registers: 1,743K

**HBM organization, bandwidth, other memory**
- "8 GB - two 4 gigabyte (GB) HBM memory stacks", split into 32 channels of 256 MB each (fetched) — [UG1371 Card Features](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1371-u50-reconfig-accel/Card-Features)
- Vitis documentation: HBM segments (pseudo channels, PCs) are 256 MB on the U50; each AXI interface should access a contiguous subset of the 32 PCs (search summary) — [UG1393 HBM Configuration and Use](https://docs.amd.com/r/2023.2-English/ug1393-vitis-application-acceleration/HBM-Configuration-and-Use)
- HBM2 capacity 8 GB; HBM2 bandwidth listed as 316 GB/s with footnote: HBM2 power is limited to 10 W from the PCIe 3.3 V rail so achievable bandwidth varies by design; nominal HBM2 bandwidth is 201 GB/s; the 316 GB/s peak was measured "under a non-PCIe-compliant specification" (fetched) — [DS965 Product Details](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details)
- FPGA fabric and HBM use separate power rails; the HBM rail has a 10 W budget "which may limit HBM performance"; designs must not overdraw either rail (fetched) — [UG1371 Card Features](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1371-u50-reconfig-accel/Card-Features), [DS965 Product Details](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details)
- Third-party listings (Datasheets.com, Avnet) quote 460 GB/s HBM bandwidth (search summary) — [Avnet U50 page](https://my.avnet.com/ebv/products/new-products/npi/2019/xilinx-xilinx-u50); contradicted by AMD's own 201 nominal / 316 peak figure in [DS965](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details)
- Structure of the same HBM generation as measured on the sibling U280: two stacks, 8 memory channels per stack, 2 pseudo channels per channel (32 total), 32 AXI channels, hardened switch built from eight mini-switches each serving four AXI channels (fetched via ar5iv) — [Shuhai, arXiv 2005.04324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04324)
- No DDR4 appears anywhere in the U50 product table or the XDMA platform memory list (only HBM and PLRAM) (fetched) — [DS965 Product Details](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details), [UG1120 U50 base_5](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1120-alveo-platforms/U50-Gen3x16-XDMA-base_5-Platform)
- 1 Gb Quad SPI flash holds the configuration (fetched) — [UG1371 Card Features](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1371-u50-reconfig-accel/Card-Features)

**I/O**
- Production U50 (A-U50-P00G-PQ-G) and U50 LV (A-U50-P00G-LV-G): 1x QSFP28 (100 GbE); network clock precision IEEE 1588 (fetched) — [DS965 Product Details](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details)
- The QSFP28 cage supports 100 GbE, 40 GbE, or 4x10/25 GbE (fetched) — [UG1371 Card Features](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1371-u50-reconfig-accel/Card-Features)
- QSFP0 uses GTY bank 131, 4 lanes; two reference-clock inputs: SYNCE_CLK on MGTREFCLK0_131 (pins N36/N37) and CLK_1588 on MGTREFCLK1_131 (pins M38/M39), both LVDS (fetched; frequencies not on this page) — [UG1371 QSFP28 Module Connector](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1371-u50-reconfig-accel/QSFP28-Module-Connector)
- Variant U50DD (A-U50DD-P00G-ES3-G): engineering-sample card with 2x SFP-DD instead of the QSFP28; "not qualified for production"; UG1371 says the two SFP-DD connectors support 4x10/25 GbE, DS965 says each SFP-DD supports 2x25G or 2x10G (search summary) — [old UG1371 PDF](https://inquirecontent2.ingrammicro.com/User-Manual/1060478627.pdf), [DigiKey listing](https://www.digikey.at/en/products/detail/amd/A-U50DD-P00G-ES3-G/10642492)
- PCIe: Gen3 x16 (8 GT/s), Gen4 x8 (16 GT/s), or CCIX 16 GT/s x8; can be split into dual x8 on hosts that support bifurcation; the hard block runs Gen4 in "compatibility mode" (PG213). U50 LV: Gen3 x16 edge but only Gen3 x4 platform shells (fetched) — [DS965 Product Details](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details)
- Other connectors: only a maintenance connector (UART; USB/JTAG configuration), I2C bus, status LEDs, SMBus voltage/current/temperature monitoring (fetched) — [UG1371 Card Features](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1371-u50-reconfig-accel/Card-Features)
- A programming cable/debug adapter is required for JTAG development access, is sold separately, and connects micro-USB to the maintenance connector (UG1377) (fetched) — [DS965 Product Details](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details)

**Power and cooling**
- Total electrical card load 75 W; passive cooling; half height, half length; 300-325 g; can run on PCIe slot power alone (fetched) — [DS965 Product Details](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details), [UG1371 Card Features](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1371-u50-reconfig-accel/Card-Features)
- Forced airflow is required whenever the card is powered; front-to-back airflow is supported (search summary) — [DS965 Airflow Direction Support](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Airflow-Direction-Support)
- AMD's Alveo debug guide: "workstations do not provide enough airflow to cool passive cards, it is easy to damage passive cards in a workstation" (search summary) — [Alveo Debug Guide, card install](https://xilinx.github.io/Alveo-Cards/master/debugging/build/html/docs/card-install)
- Vendor manual: the card is designed for a data-center server with controlled airflow; "external fan cooling airflow MUST be applied to prevent over-temperature shut-down and possible damage" (search summary) — [old UG1371 PDF](https://inquirecontent2.ingrammicro.com/User-Manual/1060478627.pdf)

**Clocking**
- XDMA Vitis platform: default accelerator (kernel) clock 300 MHz (fetched) — [UG1120 U50 base_5](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1120-alveo-platforms/U50-Gen3x16-XDMA-base_5-Platform)

- The sibling AU55C uses a 161.1328125 MHz Si5394 reference for its QSFP (search summary of an unofficial mirror of the "taxi" project; this is the U55C, not the U50) — [taxi mirror commit](https://git.byronlathi.com/bslathi19/taxi/commit/a404a2c0bae733f3175a64c1da7498b4c2b33135)

### Inferences
- Shell cost of the Gen3x16 XDMA base_5 platform (whole device minus dynamic region): LUTs 872K - 704K = ~168K (19%); registers 1,743K - 1,410K = ~333K (19%); BRAM 1,344 - 1,116 = 228 blocks (17%); URAM 640 - 544 = 96 blocks (15%); DSP 5,952 - 4,920 = 1,032 (17%). This is area reserved for the static region, not necessarily logic actually used by it.
- HBM attaches only to SLR0, so anything that touches HBM at speed should be floorplanned in SLR0; SLR1 (which also hosts the QSFP GTY quad per the xbtest U50LV checklist, "GT[0]: GTY, Quad_X0Y7 (SLR1)" — [xbtest checklist](https://xilinx.github.io/xbtest/doc/main/checklist/build/html/docs/xilinx-u50lv-gen3x4-xdma-base-2/checklist.html)) is the natural home for the network-facing front end. An Ethernet-in, HBM-history design therefore crosses the SLR boundary once.
- 20 GTY = 16 PCIe lanes + 4 QSFP lanes; there are no spare transceivers and no general-purpose I/O for a direct ADC/JESD204 connection.
- The U50 is the only mainstream Alveo UltraScale+ card with HBM but zero DDR4 (the U280 pairs 8 GB HBM with 32 GB DDR4 per [MicroRec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05894)), so 8 GB is the hard ceiling for on-card bulk storage.
- A single U50 in a Windows 11 tower/workstation needs a ducted fan or 3D-printed shroud blowing front-to-back across the heatsink; relying on case airflow risks thermal shutdown per AMD's own guidance.

### Gaps
- DS965 "Table 1" (static vs. dynamic resource split) and the U50 inlet-temperature-vs-LFM airflow table did not render; no U50-specific LFM number was verified.
- Exact on-card clock sources and frequencies (QSFP reference clock frequency, HBM reference clock, system clocks, PCIe refclk) were not retrieved; UG1371 defers to its Clocks section and the U50 XDC file on the product page. The 161.1328125 MHz figure above is for the U55C.
- Whether the XCU50 exposes exactly 32 HBM AXI ports like the XCU280 was not confirmed from a U50-specific source (only "32 pseudo channels" was).
- The claim that XCU50 is a rebadged XCVU35P rests on one blog; AMD documents only call it "XCU50".
- Whether the XDMA base_5 platform exposes the QSFP GTs to user kernels was not confirmed for the gen3x16 U50 platform (only seen for the U50LV gen3x4 platform in xbtest).

## 2. Tooling and lifecycle as of late 2026: tool versions, shells, XRT, EOL, licensing, Windows 11, open-source shells

### Takeaway
**[RECENT CHANGE]** AMD formally discontinued the U50 in XCN26004 (27 April 2026): last-time-buy closed 30 October 2026, last shipments by 2 April 2027, RMA until 2 April 2030, "No Direct Replacement". **[RECENT CHANGE]** Vivado 2026.1+ will not launch without a license and puts Alveo parts in a dedicated "Alveo tier" (free for one year to card buyers), whereas Vivado through 2025.2 covered Alveo in the licence-free Standard edition; the last U50 Vitis platform is still the April 2022 `xilinx_u50_gen3x16_xdma_5_202210_1`, XRT for Alveo cards is Linux-only (Windows is NPU-only, WSL2 has no PCI passthrough), and of the open-source shells only OpenNIC and Corundum list the U50.

### Inferences
- As of 21 November 2026 the LTB window (30 October 2026) has closed; a second or spare U50 can now only come from distributor stock or the secondary market, and the single card the user owns has RMA cover only for quality defects until 2 April 2030. There is no pin-compatible successor; the nearest living HBM Alveo is the U55C (16 GB HBM, 2x QSFP28), which Coyote/ESnet also target.
- Practical toolchain choice for one U50: (a) Vivado/Vitis 2022.1-2025.2, which need no licence for Alveo parts and which every open-source U50 shell was written against (OpenNIC: 2020.x-2022.1); or (b) Vivado 2026.x, which requires an Alveo-tier licence whose availability to someone who bought the card before 2026 is undocumented. Pinning to a 2022.x-2025.2 release is the low-risk path, and is also what the frozen `202210_1` XDMA platform expects.
- The standard U50 XDMA platform surviving the 2024.1 purge (which removed U50LV, U55N, U280 platforms) implies it still worked in the 2024.1 Vitis/XRT flow; nothing found confirms or denies v++ support for it in 2025.x/2026.x.
- Consequences of a Windows 11 workstation: (1) the XRT/Vitis acceleration flow (xbutil, xclbin loading, XDMA shell) needs a bare-metal Linux boot on the machine that physically hosts the card; WSL2 cannot see the card; a dual-boot or a second Linux box is required. (2) A pure Vivado RTL flow can be built on Windows and loaded over JTAG (needs the separately sold Alveo programming cable) or flashed, and a custom design using the XDMA/QDMA IP could talk to AMD's reference Windows drivers, but those are test-signed lab drivers, not a supported runtime. (3) OpenNIC, Corundum and Coyote all ship Linux-only kernel drivers. (4) USRP 100GbE host streaming is also Linux-only, though that is irrelevant if the radio streams straight into the U50's QSFP.
- What open shells add over the vendor Vitis flow for this project: direct RTL-level access to the 100G CMAC stream at 250/322 MHz with no XRT/OpenCL kernel wrapper in the data path, PTP timestamping (Corundum), and a host NIC interface for control/weight updates. What they lack: ready-made HBM plumbing on the U50 (Coyote has it, but not for the U50).

## 3. HBM for ML: published uses, latency/throughput vs. BRAM/URAM, and where weights should live

### Takeaway
Measured on the sibling U280, an idle HBM read costs about 107-138 ns (48-62 cycles at 450 MHz), rising to 122-171 ns through the hardened switch and more through the Vitis kernel wrapper, with roughly 13 GB/s per pseudo channel; on-chip BRAM/URAM is about three times faster per access and has no refresh stalls. Published ML designs therefore use HBM for large parallel lookup tables (recommendation embeddings) while keeping small tables and all network weights on-chip, which is the right split for a sub-microsecond to few-microsecond model on the U50.

### Cited Findings
- Shuhai (ETH Zurich, FCCM 2020) idle-access latency on the U280: HBM page hit 48 cycles (106.7 ns), page closed 55 cycles (122.2 ns), page miss 62 cycles (137.8 ns) at a 450 MHz AXI clock; DDR4 page hit 22 cycles (73.3 ns), closed 27 (89.9 ns), miss 32 (106.6 ns) at 300 MHz; HBM is about 30 ns slower than DDR4 per category (fetched via ar5iv) — [Shuhai, arXiv 2005.04324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04324)
- Shuhai throughput: 13.27 GB/s per HBM AXI channel, 32 channels, 425 GB/s total measured (450 GB/s theoretical at 450 MHz x 32 channels x 32 B); DDR4 18 GB/s per channel, 36 GB/s total (fetched via ar5iv) — [Shuhai](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04324)
- Shuhai with the HBM switch enabled: page-hit latency from AXI channels 0-3 to HBM channel 0 is 55 cycles (122.2 ns), from channels 28-31 it is 77 cycles (171.1 ns); enabling the switch adds seven cycles; AXI channels in the same mini-switch see identical latency (fetched via ar5iv) — [Shuhai](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04324)
- Shuhai access-pattern effects: address-mapping policy changes sequential throughput by almost 10x (default RGBCG policy best); small bursts give low throughput; with burst 32 / stride 4K, throughput is 6.7 GB/s for an 8K working set versus 2.4 GB/s for a 256M working set; a read that coincides with a periodic refresh (interval on the order of 7.8 us) takes significantly longer (fetched via ar5iv) — [Shuhai](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04324)
- A Vitis-flow HBM benchmark found HLS and RTL kernels have the same HBM access latency, but Vitis "will encapsulate the kernel" which increases latency, and the HBM-vs-DDR latency gap is larger under Vitis than under plain Vivado (search summary) — [arXiv 2010.08916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.08916.pdf)
- "When HLS Meets FPGA HBM" (UCLA) benchmarks U280 and Stratix 10 MX and notes latency-bound workloads such as binary search achieve much reduced effective bandwidth on both (search summary) — [arXiv 2010.06075](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2010.06075)
- A Waterloo thesis on HBM NoCs: the hardened crossbar "introduces several throughput bottlenecks"; point-to-point burst-16 access reaches 98% of theoretical throughput, global access drops throughput by 4x, and single-flit point-to-point access reaches only 43% (search summary) — [Kuttuva Prakash thesis](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/bitstream/10012/17909/3/KuttuvaPrakash_Srinirdheeshwar.pdf)
- MicroRec (ETH/Alibaba, MLSys 2021) on the U280: "Embedding tables are distributed over both on-chip memory (BRAM and URAM) and off-chip memory (HBM and DDR)"; the 32 HBM pseudo channels plus 2 DDR channels allow up to 34 concurrent table lookups; small tables go on-chip (fetched via ar5iv) — [MicroRec, arXiv 2010.05894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05894)
- MicroRec states HBM and DDR access latency is "a couple of hundreds of nanoseconds" and that on-chip access takes about 1/3 of the DDR4/HBM time because it has no read-initiation overhead (fetched via ar5iv) — [MicroRec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05894)
- MicroRec embedding-lookup latency for a 47-table model: 774 ns with HBM only, 458 ns with HBM plus Cartesian-product table merging; the paper's figures for the 98-table model are internally inconsistent (1.38/1.03 us in Table 4 versus 2.26/1.63 us in the text) (fetched via ar5iv) — [MicroRec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05894)
- MicroRec end-to-end inference latency (lookups plus a 3-hidden-layer 1024-512-256 MLP): 16.3 us (small, fp16), 22.6 us (small fp32 and large fp16), 31.0 us (large fp32); once lookups fall under 1 us "the bottleneck shifts to computation" (fetched via ar5iv) — [MicroRec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05894)
- MicroRec deliberately used a 32-bit AXI interface to HBM rather than 512-bit because wider interfaces consumed too many BRAMs and lowered the clock (fetched via ar5iv) — [MicroRec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05894)
- FleetRec (KDD 2021) uses FPGAs with HBM purely as the embedding-lookup tier in a hybrid GPU-FPGA cluster; reported 15.5-49.0x throughput over a CPU baseline (search summary; authors' own evaluation) — [FleetRec](https://wenqijiang.github.io/files/2021_KDD_FleetRec.pdf)
- SKA-Low correlator on Alveo U55C relies on on-card HBM to chain filterbank, wavefront correction and correlation on one device, i.e. HBM as a streaming sample/delay buffer (search summary) — [Hampson et al., JATIS 2022](https://photonicsforenergy.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/Journal-of-Astronomical-Telescopes-Instruments-and-Systems/volume-8/issue-1/011018/Square-Kilometre-Array-Low-Atomic-commercial-off-the-shelf-correlator/10.1117/1.JATIS.8.1.011018.full)
- AMD ships an HBM bandwidth example for Vitis platforms (search result title only) — [Vitis Accel Examples: HBM Bandwidth](https://xilinx.github.io/Vitis_Accel_Examples/2023.1/html/hbm_bandwidth.html)
- U50-specific constraint: nominal HBM bandwidth 201 GB/s, peak 316 GB/s, limited by the 10 W HBM rail (fetched) — [DS965 Product Details](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/Product-Details)

### Inferences
- A single HBM fetch (about 0.11-0.17 us before Vitis wrapper overhead, plus occasional refresh stalls) consumes 10-17% of a 1 us budget and adds jitter. For sub-microsecond inference, no HBM access can sit on the per-sample critical path more than once, and ideally not at all; for a 2-5 us budget one or two pipelined HBM lookups per inference are affordable (MicroRec did a full lookup round in under 0.5-0.8 us).
- HBM delivery rate is also too low to feed a wide datapath: one pseudo channel moves 32 bytes per 450 MHz cycle (16 sixteen-bit weights per cycle), so all 32 channels together supply about 512 sixteen-bit weights per cycle, roughly a tenth of what the 4,920-5,952 DSP slices could consume each clock. The U50's 10 W HBM cap (201 GB/s nominal vs. the U280's 425 GB/s measured) makes this worse.
- Recommended placement for the stated project (small, runtime-updated network): active weights in registers/LUTRAM/BRAM/URAM next to the multipliers; HBM used for (a) a deep raw-sample history / pre-trigger ring buffer, (b) recorded-dataset replay, (c) a replay buffer or shadow copies of weights for on-line training and atomic weight swaps, (d) large lookup tables (templates, calibration, embeddings) fetched with pipelined, look-ahead addressing.
- Runtime weight updates into BRAM/URAM are cheap (one write port per memory, a few ns per word), and an A/B double-buffer swap avoids tearing; HBM is the natural staging area for the "next" weight set arriving over PCIe or Ethernet.
- All published HBM latency numbers are from the U280 (XCU280) at 450 MHz; the U50's XCU50 uses the same HBM architecture, but a design clocked at the 300 MHz platform default will see the same cycle counts with longer wall-clock latency (48 cycles at 300 MHz is 160 ns) unless the HBM AXI domain is run faster.

### Gaps
- No HBM latency/throughput measurement made on a U50 itself was found; all figures are U280 (and the U50's power-limited bandwidth suggests it would be somewhat worse).
- No published design was found that stores neural-network weights in HBM for a low-latency model, or that uses HBM as an on-line-learning replay buffer; the uses found are embedding tables (MicroRec/FleetRec) and streaming sample buffers (SKA-Low).
- BRAM/URAM read latency in nanoseconds was not sourced from a primary document; the only cited comparison is MicroRec's "about 1/3 of HBM/DDR".
- Exact refresh-induced worst-case HBM latency is shown only in a Shuhai figure and was not extracted.

## 4. Signal ingest: realistic ways to get real-time signal data into a card with no ADC/FMC

### Takeaway
The only high-rate real-time input on a U50 is its single QSFP28: packetized ADC samples over 100GbE (or 4x10/25GbE) from an external digitizer such as an RFSoC board or a USRP X410/X440, received by the hardened 100G CMAC with a free licence; PCIe from the host is the alternative for recorded data and control but puts an OS in the loop. Open designs exist for the transmit side (CASPER RFSoC 4x2 100GbE, Strathclyde QSFP offload, rfnoc-difi) and Alveo cards are used at scale as 100GbE-fed signal processors (SKA-Low, CRACO), but I found no published U50-specific real-time signal-processing design and no measured CMAC-to-inference latency figure.

### Inferences
- Ingest options ranked for the stated goal (real-time faint-signal detection, very low latency):
  1. **External digitizer -> 100GbE -> U50 QSFP28 -> CMAC -> depacketizer -> model.** Capacity: using the CASPER framing (8,298 bytes on the wire plus 20 bytes preamble/IFG per 2,048 complex 16+16-bit samples), a single 100GbE link carries about 1.5 M packets/s, i.e. about 3.0 GS/s complex or 6 GS/s real 16-bit samples. This is the only path that keeps the host OS out of the data plane.
  2. **Same link at 4x10/25GbE** for cheaper digitizers (e.g. X410 X4_200 image, 4x10GbE): about 1/4 to 1/10 the sample rate, same architecture.
  3. **PCIe (XDMA/QDMA) from the host**: fine for replay of recorded datasets, weight updates, and result readback; real-time ingest this way inherits host scheduling jitter (tens of microseconds is typical of host-in-the-loop paths, cf. the 65 us worst-case RTT measured for a DPDK USRP loop).
  4. **Replay from HBM**: load a recording once over PCIe, then play it into the model at full rate with deterministic timing. 8 GB holds about 2.1 G complex 16-bit samples (4 bytes each), i.e. about 4.3 s at 500 MS/s or about 43 s at 50 MS/s; one or two pseudo channels (about 13 GB/s each on the U280) are far more than enough bandwidth.
- The single QSFP28 is a real constraint with USRPs: an X410 at full 400 MHz bandwidth needs one 100GbE link per 2 channels and an X440 CG_1600 needs one link per channel, so one U50 can ingest at most 2 full-rate X410 channels or 1 X440 1.6 GHz channel. A 500 MS/s sc16 channel is 16 Gb/s, so the limit is the radio's link mapping, not raw capacity.
- UHD's host API is not needed (or usable) on the FPGA side: the U50 must parse the radio's wire format (CHDR-over-UDP, or DIFI/VITA-49 via rfnoc-difi) in RTL, and something must still configure the radio. An RFSoC running CASPER or Strathclyde-style firmware is the more open transmitter because the packet format is trivial (sequence counter plus raw samples) and under the user's control.
- Latency budget of the Ethernet path is dominated by packetization, not the wire: filling a 2,048-sample packet takes 4.1 us at 500 MS/s and 1.0 us at 2 GS/s; a sub-microsecond end-to-end target requires much smaller packets (e.g. 64-256 samples) at the cost of header overhead, or sample-synchronous processing that starts on the first word of each packet. RS-FEC, which the CMAC and the CASPER design enable by default, adds further fixed latency that I could not source a number for.
- "Detecting missed events" with a pre-trigger window maps naturally onto HBM as a multi-second raw-sample ring buffer written from the CMAC stream in SLR0 while the model runs on the live stream.

### Gaps
- No source gave the receive-path latency of the UltraScale+ 100G CMAC with or without RS-FEC, nor an end-to-end ADC-to-FPGA-over-100GbE latency for any RFSoC or USRP design.
- No measured XDMA or QDMA small-transfer latency/jitter numbers on an Alveo card were found; AMD only says PCIe latency is "high".
- No published or open-source design was found that uses the U50 specifically for real-time signal processing, SDR, or radio astronomy; all examples are U55C/U280/U250.
- No documentation was found for an officially supported way to stream USRP samples directly to a non-UHD UDP endpoint other than the rfnoc-difi out-of-tree block; the Ettus X410 knowledge-base page returned HTTP 403.
- VITA-49/VRT and DIFI receiver cores for Alveo, and ZCU111/ZCU216-specific 100GbE designs (those boards' Ethernet options), were not researched in depth; the CASPER and Strathclyde designs found both target the RFSoC 4x2.
- Sample bit width on the wire for USRP 100GbE (sc16 assumed) was not confirmed in the portion of the UHD manual read.

## 5. Capacity implications: weights in BRAM+URAM, MACs per clock, and model sizes others have fit

### Takeaway
On-chip memory can hold on the order of 25-30 million 8-bit or 12-15 million 16-bit weights, and the DSP slices give roughly 4,900-5,950 multiply-accumulates per clock (about 1.5 million MACs inside a 1 us window at 300 MHz), so the binding limits for a runtime-updatable, microsecond-class model are multipliers and memory-port bandwidth, not storage. Published nanosecond-class FPGA networks (50-80 ns, thousands of parameters) bake weights into logic at synthesis; the closest published runtime-weight comparator is MicroRec's roughly 1-1.5 M-weight MLP at 16-31 us on a larger U280, while Vitis-AI DPU models on the U50 itself run at milliseconds.

### Cited Findings

- hls4ml autoencoders for LHC anomaly detection: inference "in as little as 80 ns using less than 3% of the logic resources in the Xilinx Virtex VU9P FPGA" (search summary) — [arXiv 2108.03986](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03986)

### Inferences
- Weight storage (DS965 "Mb" figures are binary megabits: 1,344 x 36,864 = 49.5 x 10^6 bits; 640 x 294,912 = 188.7 x 10^6 bits):
  - Whole device, BRAM + URAM = 238 x 10^6 bits: about 29.8 M 8-bit or 14.9 M 16-bit weights (BRAM alone 6.2 M / 3.1 M; URAM alone 23.6 M / 11.8 M).
  - Under the XDMA shell (1,116 BRAM + 544 URAM = 201.6 x 10^6 bits): about 25.2 M 8-bit or 12.6 M 16-bit weights (BRAM 5.1 M / 2.6 M; URAM 20.1 M / 10.0 M).
  - These are ceilings with every memory block spent on weights; buffers, FIFOs, the CMAC/DMA plumbing and activation storage will take a share, so planning on 50-70% is more realistic.
  - HBM adds 8.6 x 10^9 8-bit or 4.3 x 10^9 16-bit values, but only at about 0.1-0.2 us per access (section 3), so it suits snapshots, history and replay rather than hot weights.
- Multiply-accumulate budget: one MAC per DSP slice per clock gives 5,952 MAC/clock for the whole device or 4,920 in the XDMA dynamic region. At 300 MHz that is at most about 1.8 x 10^12 (device) or 1.5 x 10^12 (dynamic region) MAC/s. Within a latency budget of T at 300 MHz the upper bound is about 1.5 M MACs for 1 us, 3 M for 2 us, 7.4 M for 5 us, before subtracting pipeline depth, packet parsing and activation logic; half of that is a prudent planning number. Two 8-bit multiplies can be packed into one DSP48E2 with known INT8 techniques (AMD white paper WP486, not re-verified in this session), roughly doubling the 8-bit figure; small-bit-width multipliers can also be built from LUTs.
- Memory-port bandwidth roughly matches the DSP count: a URAM is 72 bits wide with two ports (device architecture per UG573, not re-verified in this session), i.e. 9 sixteen-bit weights per cycle, so 544 URAMs can deliver about 4,900 sixteen-bit weights per cycle, which is about what 4,920 DSPs can consume. A fully time-multiplexed design is therefore balanced; a fully unrolled single-cycle layer needs its weights in registers or LUTRAM instead.
- The nanosecond-class results (hls4ml at 50-80 ns, LogicNets at 6 ns) rely on weights fixed at synthesis and folded into LUT logic. A model whose weights change at runtime cannot use that trick: it needs real multipliers and writable weight storage, which pushes it onto the DSP/memory budget above. Under that constraint a fully parallel network is limited to a few thousand weights evaluated per pipeline stage (about 5K sixteen-bit MACs/clock), e.g. a dense network of a few thousand to low tens of thousands of weights at tens of nanoseconds, or up to roughly a million weights at a few microseconds with time-multiplexing.
- MicroRec is the best published sizing reference for a runtime-loadable MLP of that upper scale: its (1024, 512, 256) MLP implies about 1.0 M weights (352-wide input) to 1.5 M weights (876-wide input) and took 16-31 us end-to-end at 120-140 MHz using 4,625-5,193 DSPs and 485K-584K LUTs on a U280. The fp16 configuration (4,625 DSP, 485K LUT) is at the edge of what the U50's XDMA dynamic region offers (4,920 DSP, 704K LUT), so a model of that size is about the practical ceiling on a U50, and a sub-microsecond target implies something one to two orders of magnitude smaller (tens of thousands of weights) or aggressively quantized.
- The Vitis-AI DPU route is a poor fit: it delivers millisecond latency on the U50 and is no longer supported for this card after Vitis AI 1.4.x; a custom RTL/HLS datapath (hls4ml-, FINN- or hand-written style) is required for microsecond-class latency.
- Cross-SLR routing limited MicroRec to 120-140 MHz at high utilization; on the two-SLR U50 a small model confined to one SLR should hold the 300 MHz platform clock, which matters because every latency estimate above scales with clock period.

### Gaps
- No published design was found that reports a small, runtime-weight-updated (on-line learning) network on a U50 with latency and resource numbers; all sizing above is derived by arithmetic from device resources and from designs on other cards.
- No hls4ml or FINN result with LUT/DSP/latency tables specifically for the U50 was retrieved (U50 appears as a target in two hls4ml studies, but the tables seen are for the U250); FINN on U50 was not found.
- DSP48E2 dual-INT8 packing and the URAM/BRAM port widths are standard device knowledge (WP486, UG573) but were not opened and verified in this session.
- Achievable clock frequency for a mixed CMAC + HBM + inference design on the U50 is unverified; 300 MHz is only the XDMA platform's default kernel clock.
