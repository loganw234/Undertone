## The closed form, from P2

Write sigma for the noise's standard deviation and E for the pulse's energy. By P2 the correlation g is Gaussian with standard deviation sigma sqrt(E) under both hypotheses, with mean 0 when the pulse is absent and mean E when it is present. Q(x) is the probability that a standard Gaussian value exceeds x.

1. The detector decides "present" when g > t. With the pulse absent, g / (sigma sqrt(E)) is standard Gaussian, so Pfa = Q(t / (sigma sqrt(E))), and t = sigma sqrt(E) Q^-1(Pfa).
2. With the pulse present, (g - E) / (sigma sqrt(E)) is standard Gaussian, so Pd = Q((t - E) / (sigma sqrt(E))) = Q(Q^-1(Pfa) - sqrt(E) / sigma).

sqrt(E) is the pulse's norm, so sqrt(E) / sigma is D8's d.

## The convention, so that two forms do not meet unannounced

Row P4's chapter writes the matched filter in the **convolution form**, y[i] = sum of c[k] x[i - k], where the coefficients c are the signal **reversed in time**. This project writes every filter in the **correlation form**, score[i] = sum of coeff[k] x[i - (T-1) + k], coefficients **oldest sample first**, where the matched filter's coefficients are the pulse **in order** (the lead's seam conventions of 2026-11-23, `docs/model/conventions.md` on the round branch). They are the same filter. Rows S1 to S3 and row P1 write the statistic as a plain correlation, sum of x[n] s[n], which is the correlation form at the one index where the filter lies wholly on the pulse.
